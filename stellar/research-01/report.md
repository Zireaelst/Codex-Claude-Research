# What to build with Stellar: venture investigation

Research checked 20 October 2026. Research and analysis only. No organizations were contacted, services purchased, contracts deployed, transactions made, or applications submitted.

**Best company thesis to validate:** a payment delivery and recovery operations platform for existing crossborder payout businesses using multiple providers. Start with Stellar USDC flows; help operators resolve delayed delivery, holds, returns and misallocated refunds across the chain, payout provider, beneficiary and internal ledger. Grow into the operational system for payment obligations, recovery approvals, partner performance and treasury availability.

**Best backup:** a redemption and lifecycle operations platform for distributors already offering multiple tokenized cash products. It coordinates investor eligibility, orders, custody, issuer dealing cycles, redemptions and actual cash availability across providers.

**Main reason I may be wrong:** both may be modules of existing financial platforms rather than independently purchased companies. Incumbents already provide reconciliation, workflows, information requests, refunds and accounting. The unvalidated gap is ownership and resolution of cases across providers. There is no verified proprietary distribution advantage for this founder.

**Investment verdict:** the evidence supports a multichain business with Stellar as an important production rail. It does not establish a uniquely Stellar, already validated venture opportunity. I would authorize customer discovery and a tightly scoped pilot, not a broad platform build or a claim of product-market fit. Privacy and agents are useful possible capabilities; neither is the demand thesis. An Instaward is a separate execution opportunity, considered only after selecting the company hypothesis.

## How to read the evidence

- **Verified fact (F):** a current primary document, repository, price schedule, filing or job posting establishes the stated behavior or fact. Documentation is not independent proof of reliability or usage.
- **Vendor claim (V):** provider statements about scale, traction, benefits, customers or service quality, including vendor-published case studies.
- **User anecdote (A):** a public individual account, unverified and potentially commercially motivated.
- **Reasonable inference (I):** an analytical conclusion from the cited facts.
- **Unvalidated hypothesis (H):** the proposed product, customer gap, price, acquisition path or business economics.

All proposed-company pricing and numerical go/no-go thresholds below are hypotheses or decision criteria, not observed demand or forecasts. No TAM, revenue, active-user or transaction estimates are inferred from tokenized AUM, grant awards, wallet installations or aggregate chain activity. “Production documented” means primary sources publish production availability/deployments; it does not mean this research independently exercised the service. No mainnet contract/RPC census or buyer interviews were performed.

## A. Verified Stellar capability and maturity map

All platform and program facts in this report were checked on **20 October 2026**. Dates below additionally identify relevant source releases. Where sources conflict, the more conservative maturity classification is used.

### Assets, execution and accounts

| Surface | Usable today and maturity | Product implication |
|---|---|---|
| Classic assets | Production native issuance, payments, orderbook/path payments and issuer controls. Asset identity is **code plus issuer**, not ticker. SEP-41 contract tokens are a different model. [Asset comparison](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/anatomy-of-an-asset). | Reuse classic USDC payments for ordinary transfers; issuing another token is not a business. |
| Soroban and SAC | Production smart contracts. SAC exposes a classic asset through the contract token interface without creating a separate wrapped asset. [SAC documentation](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/stellar-asset-contract). | A fund existing on Stellar does not imply any vault or lending contract may hold it. | Current protocol | The official version registry records Protocol 28 on mainnet; Protocol 26 went live May 6 and Protocol 27 July 8, 2026. [Version registry](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/networks/software-versions). | Avoid designs based on obsolete pre-Soroban or pre-2026 limitations. |
| Trustlines/reserves | Classic accounts opt into issued assets. Current base reserve is 0.5 XLM; ordinary account minimum is two base reserves, plus subentries such as trustlines. [Accounts](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/stellar-data-structures/accounts). Clawback must be enabled appropriately; it is not a universal reversal button. [Issuer controls](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/control-asset-access). | Separate permission to hold, permission to transfer, legal eligibility and recovery powers. | Smart accounts | Contract accounts implement `__check_auth` for passkeys, signers, allowlists, time rules or spending limits. [Contract accounts, updated Oct 6](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/contract-accounts). | Use only where policy enforcement justifies contract/security/support complexity. | Sponsorship | Production reserve sponsorship can cover accounts, trustlines and other subentries; a fully sponsored new account can start with zero own XLM. Fee bumps separately let another account pay transaction fees. [Reserves](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/transactions/sponsored-reserves), [fee bumps](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/transactions/fee-bump-transactions). | “Gasless” UX has a sponsor cost and abuse surface. Paying a fee does not authorize business spending. |
| FX and settlement | Path payments require actual liquidity and an executable path. [Path payments](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/transactions/path-payments). | Benchmark net fiat delivered, quote expiry and failure recovery; low chain fees do not remove FX, bank or cash costs. |

### Privacy: exact property, visibility and production limit

| System | What it can hide / who still sees | Maturity and real-asset decision |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary Stellar / Soroban | Transactions and contract data are public. Access control on a function does not encrypt ledger data. | Production transparent financial rails; keep personal and commercial records offchain. [Privacy overview](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/apps/privacy). |
| Stellar Private Payments, Nethermind | Shielded-pool internal counterparties and amounts; entry/exit amounts and addresses remain public. Optional global auditor view key exposes pool-wide activity; user disclosure is currently note-scoped, not complete source-of-funds proof. ASP controls govern key admission/exclusion. Timing, tiny pools and app metadata can correlate edges (I). | **Unaudited developer preview, testnet only, no real assets.** [Preview and visibility model](https://stellar.org/blog/developers/developer-preview-stellar-private-payments). No safe mainnet payroll assumption. |
| Confidential Tokens, OpenZeppelin/Nethermind | Internal balances/amounts hidden; sender and recipient addresses remain public. In the **wrapper model**, entry/exit expose underlying transfers. Auditor access/selective disclosure and freeze/policy hooks are preview capabilities; see the later issuer design below. | **Testnet developer preview; current docs still say unaudited and unsuitable for production/real assets.** Contract/verifier audits were underway in the preview notice. [Preview](https://stellar.org/blog/developers/developer-preview-confidential-tokens-on-stellar), [current warning](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/apps/privacy). A normal OZ library audit does not cover this entire system. |
| Native ZK primitives / verifiers | BN254, BLS12-381 and Poseidon-family facilities support proof verification. What is hidden depends on the circuit and application, not the presence of a verifier. | Production cryptographic infrastructure; not an audited private financial application. Circuits, proof generation, keys, recovery and consuming contracts still require review. [ZK documentation](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/apps/zk). |
| Moonlight | Separate provider-mediated UTXO/bundling design; trusted providers see their users' records. Primary technical meeting says it does not currently use ZK. | Core audit exists, but the demonstrated flow was testnet. Secondary mainnet descriptions conflict; commercial real-asset mainnet operation was not established here. [Aug 27 technical notes](https://developers.stellar.org/meetings/2026/08/27), [Runtime Verification audit](https://runtimeverification.com/public-report/moonlight-audit). Audit scope is core channel contracts, not every provider/wallet/backend. |
| Hypertron | Vendor describes B2B/treasury/agent operations and shielded payments. | Docs disclose testnet, no audit, single-coordinator setup and replaceable verifier keys. Not a real-money dependency. [Protocol disclosures](https://www.hypertron.space/docs/protocol). |

The later **issuer-controlled confidential stablecoin design** specifies scoped dual auditors: the recipient's auditor sees the incoming amount; the sender's auditor sees the amount sent and remaining balance. Administrative issuer authority alone does not provide decryption or unilateral recovery; intervention requires coordination with auditing roles. Its separate standalone issuance model can hide mint/burn amounts and hold supply as commitments, unlike transparent-asset wrapping. This is architecture/preview evidence, not a verified audited mainnet issuer product. [Later architecture](https://stellar.org/blog/developers/practical-confidential-stablecoins-an-issuer-controlled-architecture). Neither model automatically conceals wallet/application metadata or supplies legal compliance (I).

**Commercial consequence (I):** privacy may be necessary for some employers or investment firms, but current Stellar SPP/Confidential Token maturity cannot meet that production requirement. First determine whether confidential addresses, amounts or business relationships are actually blocking a buyer. Ordinary encrypted business records are feasible today, while onchain amounts remain visible. Do not sell these as equivalent properties.

### Agents and MetaMask: three separate products

Stellar's current CLI for agents supports machine-readable token operations, allowances and unsigned drafts. Contract accounts/policy signers can enforce scoped permissions, while a full unrestricted key defeats that architecture. A token allowance is not a correct invoice, vendor decision or evidence of service fulfillment. [Agent CLI](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tools/cli/agent-cli), [OZ policy framework](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tools/openzeppelin-contracts).

x402 on Stellar uses token/Soroban authorization flows. The Built on Stellar/OZ facilitator documents mainnet and testnet endpoints; Coinbase facilitator support in Stellar docs is testnet. MPP documents one-time charges and prefunded sessions with signed cumulative commitments. These are usable payment infrastructures, not privacy or demand evidence. [x402](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/agentic-payments/x402), [facilitator](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/agentic-payments/x402/built-on-stellar), [MPP](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/agentic-payments/mpp). Rail402 publishes a facilitator, marketplace/SDK/MCP and mainnet examples; ROZO's router is documented as mainnet. Catalog size and provider-reported counts do not establish recurring independent buyers. [Rail402](https://rail402.dev/), [ROZO demonstration](https://developers.stellar.org/meetings/2026/04/23).

## C. Fifteen company theses and explicit eliminations

All entries below describe proposed full products (H). Competitors and enabling capabilities are documented in A–B. Confidence separates category pain from confidence in the **new entrant's gap**. The business becomes more than its first feature only if it owns the repeated workflow and purchasing relationship described.

### 1. Payment delivery and recovery operations — advance first

**Customer/buyer:** Head of Payment Operations/COO at a regulated payout business with two or more providers; analysts are daily users. **Job/workaround:** resolve delayed/returned payouts using provider dashboards, ledger queries and support tickets. **Product/repeat:** obligation ledger, beneficiary evidence collection, case ownership, SLAs, approved recovery/reissue, liability reconciliation and client proof of delivery. **Stellar/value:** reconcile native asset/anchor events precisely and reduce recurring delivery failures without changing the customer's rail contracts. **Revenue/distribution:** subscription plus throughput; founder sales through operator directories, later payout implementers. **Dependencies:** provider records/data rights, licensed execution partners, security/outsourcing review. **Multichain:** necessary beyond launch. **Substitute:** Formance/Modern Treasury + internal case tooling. **Failure:** incumbent already closes cases well or integrations remain bespoke. **Confidence:** high problem existence, low-to-medium independent gap. Advance to artifact-based interviews.

### 2. Distributor RWA redemption and lifecycle operations — best backup

**Customer/buyer:** investment app/wallet distributor COO with multiple issuers; onboarding, treasury and support users. **Job/workaround:** coordinate eligibility, expiring orders, fund cutoffs, custody and cash exits through issuer portals/email. **Product/repeat:** investor service desk from approved account to subscription, valuation, redemption and cash receipt, with reconciled reporting. **Stellar/value:** handle classic/SAC and Soroban product differences plus local ramps; offer cross-issuer continuity. **Revenue/distribution:** annual SaaS/connector fee through distributors and administrators. **Dependencies:** issuer/TA APIs and permission, data agreement; regulated party retains investment/transfer decisions. **Multichain:** essential. **Substitute:** Spiko/issuer support + Tokenistry/TRES. **Failure:** one issuer handles everything or residual cases are trivial. **Confidence:** medium pain, low independent purchase intent. Advance only with current multi-issuer buyers.

### 3. Multi-entity cash governance and treasury operations — conditional third

**Customer/buyer:** CFO/treasurer of a holding group with many operating entities; local controllers. **Job/workaround:** cash floors, approvals, bank/fund placements and consolidated close across portals/spreadsheets. **Product/repeat:** per-entity forecasts/policies, approved sweeps/redemptions, obligation-aware cash availability and audit reports. **Stellar/value:** useful when treasury or payment destinations already use Stellar assets; protocol is less important to a fiat-only group. **Revenue/distribution:** group subscription plus entity seats, sold through accountants/TMS implementers. **Dependencies:** contracted investment provider, bank data, custody, entity eligibility; no unlicensed discretionary allocation. **Multichain:** yes, and bank-first. **Substitute:** Spiko Direct/Canary, incumbent TMS or bank. **Failure:** strong existing solution and no reason for Stellar. **Confidence:** medium category demand from named case, low differentiated entry. Keep for diligence, not Stellar-first build.

### 4. Service-export project-to-cash platform — conditional finalist, defer

**Customer/buyer:** agency owner/finance lead with repeated crossborder projects; client approver is a second stakeholder. **Job/workaround:** SOWs, deposits, scope changes, delivery acceptance and partial collections across email/project tools/bank. **Product/repeat:** contract/change-order records, funded milestones, acceptance evidence, mediation and reconciled fiat/stablecoin receipts. **Stellar/value:** inexpensive programmable releases; reuse Trustless Work rather than sell escrow itself. **Revenue/distribution:** agency subscription and release fee via agency accountants/associations. **Dependencies:** enforceable terms, dispute service, permitted escrow/payment structure, ramps. **Multichain:** buyer fiat first; other wallets later. **Substitute:** Upwork/Escrow.com + project software. **Failure:** clients refuse prefunding; subjective disputes cost more than fees. **Confidence:** medium generic pain, low stablecoin necessity. Defer until both parties demonstrate willingness to switch.

### 5. Humanitarian field disbursement operations — eliminate as initial venture

**Customer/buyer:** NGO country finance/program director. **Job/workaround:** approved recipient lists, restricted budgets, cash/mobile payouts and donor evidence across implementing partners. **Product/repeat:** eligibility casework, distribution planning, safe recipient communications, failed-cash-out resolution and donor reports. **Stellar/value:** USDC/SDP and cash links can reduce some delivery friction. **Revenue/distribution:** organization/program SaaS, procurement via aid implementers. **Dependencies:** local permissions, beneficiary data protection, cash partner, humanitarian compliance and safeguarding. **Multichain:** donor/field rails dictate it. **Substitute:** existing cash-assistance systems, SDP integrators and bank/mobile money. **Failure:** long procurement, sensitive access, funding cycles and small independent software budget. **Confidence:** plausible repeated need, weak founder access and purchase evidence. Eliminate now; a funded deployment is not proof of a scalable vendor market.

### 6. Cash-network liquidity and recovery operations — eliminate now

**Customer/buyer:** wallet cash-operations lead or licensed remittance treasury manager. **Job/workaround:** agent cash availability, prefunding, limits, queues and unclaimed transfers through partner systems/manual escalations. **Product/repeat:** location-aware cash promises, liquidity forecasting, failed pickup cases and daily settlements. **Stellar/value:** established USDC cash rail, but MoneyGram now supports Solana too. **Revenue/distribution:** wallet subscription and permitted service fee through existing operators. **Dependencies:** partner access, agent liquidity data, local licensed institutions and potentially credit capital. **Multichain:** necessary. **Substitute:** MoneyGram's own stack and Arf/Huma-backed operators. **Failure:** data inaccessible or liquidity control remains entirely with partner. **Confidence:** medium structural problem, low accessible differentiation. Eliminate without an operator relationship and actual cash availability feed.

### 7. Full contractor workforce/payroll company — reject generic entry

**Customer/buyer:** finance/people lead of a distributed business. **Job/workaround:** contracts, worker classification, invoices, payroll, tax and local delivery via several tools. **Product/repeat:** worker portal, compliant engagement, recurring pay, exceptions, reporting and support. **Stellar/value:** one low-cost settlement rail; sponsor onboarding where needed. **Revenue/distribution:** per-active-worker/month through agencies and workforce partners. **Dependencies:** workforce legal/tax coverage, KYC, licensed ramps/custody arrangement. **Multichain:** yes. **Substitute:** Deel/Rise, Airtm, Request and dolphinze. **Failure:** startup cannot beat legal coverage, installed distribution or support. **Confidence:** high job/budget, low entrant differentiation. Reject a cheaper Stellar payroll clone.

### 8. Importer trade collections, FX and working-capital desk — reject broad version

**Customer/buyer:** controller of an importer paying recurring foreign suppliers. **Job/workaround:** orders, invoices, funding documents, FX quotes, staged payments and reconciliation through bank/FX broker/sheets. **Product/repeat:** shipment-linked obligations, exact-net quotes, approvals, supplier records and landed-cost accounting, with finance through a partner later. **Stellar/value:** useful corridor settlement if the final fiat quote wins. **Revenue/distribution:** software + transparent permitted service spread through trade accountants/logistics software. **Dependencies:** licensed collections/FX/payouts, documentary compliance and credit partner. **Multichain:** yes. **Substitute:** Airwallex/Wise, CashAbroad. **Failure:** no landed-cost advantage or suppliers refuse new payment paths. **Confidence:** medium need, low differentiated channel. Reject without a specific corridor and buyer documentation advantage.

### 9. Embedded local-government-bond savings for an existing worker channel — eliminate without channel

**Customer/buyer:** payroll/vertical-software platform; workers are recurring end users. **Job/workaround:** preserve earnings and meet bills using bank deposits/cash. **Product/repeat:** consented salary allocation, eligible bond exposure, NAV/tax statements, redemption and bill cash-out. **Stellar/value:** small transfers and issuer/ramp inventory. **Revenue/distribution:** contracted issuer revenue share plus B2B software; distribution must come from the platform. **Dependencies:** securities distributor agreement, local eligibility, custody, KYC/tax and redemption service. **Multichain:** yes. **Substitute:** Etherfuse integrations, Spiko Embedded, OpenTrade/Littio and banks. **Failure:** low balances and immediate cash-out erase revenue, acquisition and compliance overwhelm margins. **Confidence:** medium category, low accessible channel. A $500 balance at a hypothetical 30bp annual fee earns only $1.50 gross/year. Eliminate without committed distribution.

### 10. Permissioned collateral liquidation network — eliminate for this founder now

**Customer/buyer:** RWA lending operator/risk head. **Job/workaround:** maintain eligible liquidators, prices, counterparty credit and exit procedures using legal agreements and OTC relationships. **Product/repeat:** approved counterparty network, capacity checks, stress monitoring, settlement and recovery operations. **Stellar/value:** controlled assets plus Soroban settlement. **Revenue/distribution:** retainer and permitted execution/liquidity fees through issuer/lender mandates. **Dependencies:** issuer approvals, broker/custodian/credit partners, oracles, legally enforceable collateral and actual capital. **Multichain:** essential. **Substitute:** Ascend and established permissioned lending ecosystems. **Failure:** no eligible funded buyer when collateral must be sold. **Confidence:** high technical need, low founder accessibility. Eliminate; an automated liquidation contract does not supply liquidity or legal rights.

### 11. Issuer servicing and shareholder operations suite — reject generic version

**Customer/buyer:** small asset-manager COO/fund administrator. **Job/workaround:** subscriptions, ownership register, distributions, freezes/recovery, statements and audits via TA/admin systems. **Product/repeat:** complete issuer and investor portals, lifecycle controls, integrations and regulated-operator workflows. **Stellar/value:** native controls and low-value distribution. **Revenue/distribution:** per-fund annual SaaS via administrators. **Dependencies:** authoritative share register, transfer-agent arrangement, issuer keys/permissions, securities and custody counsel. **Multichain:** yes. **Substitute:** Tokeny/Securitize/Spydra and in-house TA software. **Failure:** core fiduciary work retained and incumbent bundle already covers it. **Confidence:** high ongoing job, low whitespace. Reject without a narrowly unsupported product class and paying issuer; do not reduce to an issuance wizard.

### 12. Confidential payroll and supplier-finance platform — eliminate on current maturity

**Customer/buyer:** controller of a stablecoin-paying employer. **Job/workaround:** approve sensitive payroll/supplier runs using private bank tools or visible onchain wallets. **Product/repeat:** onboarding, invoices/rosters, approvals, confidential settlement, selective audit reporting, recovery and local cash-out. **Stellar/value:** eventual privacy composition with existing stablecoins and ramps. **Revenue/distribution:** employer/recipient subscription through payroll/accounting channels. **Dependencies:** audited production privacy, issuer compatibility, recovery/view-key governance, workforce/payment legal structure. **Multichain:** likely. **Substitute:** private fiat payroll and competing confidential finance products. **Failure:** current previews cannot safely carry real assets or public counterparties still violate the buyer's needs. **Confidence:** medium privacy rationale, low verified WTP. Eliminate today. Reopen only when privacy blocks a documented paying workflow and production dependencies are independently established.

### 13. Agent procurement and spend-control company — reject generic version after diligence

**Customer/buyer:** AI-platform lead plus controller at a company with real paid automated API/compute procurement. **Job/workaround:** provision vendors/budgets, review bills, investigate failed delivery and revoke spending using cards/prepaid credits. **Product/repeat:** procurement contracts, delegated mandates, usage-to-delivery receipts, refunds, approvals and ERP close. **Stellar/value:** inexpensive machine payments and programmable policy; no exclusive demand advantage. **Revenue/distribution:** subscription tied to governed spend via finance/platform teams. **Dependencies:** seller evidence, wallet policy, billing/card/PSP partnerships and concurrent-spend controls. **Multichain:** mandatory. **Substitute:** Ramp, Coinbase/Privy/Crossmint, Rail402/policy tools. **Failure:** demo traffic or incumbent solves it. **Confidence:** high technical supply, low unmet budget. Reject broad “Ramp for agents”; monitor a proven vertical fulfillment problem.

### 14. Paid-API merchant revenue operations — eliminate until real sellers qualify

**Customer/buyer:** owner/RevOps of an API provider selling repeatedly to unrelated agents. **Job/workaround:** usage metering, entitlement, receipts, tax, refunds and treasury via Stripe/prepaid credits. **Product/repeat:** seller billing/fulfillment backoffice and customer dispute workflow, with x402/MPP as rails. **Stellar/value:** small transfers where they beat prepaid batching. **Revenue/distribution:** subscription + processing through existing API gateways. **Dependencies:** facilitator, merchant contracts, usage integrity, refund authority and licensed custody if holding balances. **Multichain:** yes. **Substitute:** Stripe/MPP, Rail402/ROZO and existing metered billing. **Failure:** negligible independent recurring sales; processing revenue too small. **Confidence:** low buyer demand. At hypothetical $20k/month processing and 1%, gross revenue is $200 before costs. Eliminate until at least three sellers show paid demand and material operational pain.

### 15. Migrant household earn/spend/remittance account — reject generic wallet

**Customer/buyer:** household repeatedly receiving foreign income; employer/channel may pay B2B fees. **Job/workaround:** remittance receipt, savings, household budgets, bills and emergency cash via wallet/card/bank. **Product/repeat:** family permissions, recurring bill funding, predictable local cash access, statements/support and eligible savings. **Stellar/value:** cash rail and native stablecoins; MetaMask may reduce setup for some senders. **Revenue/distribution:** modest subscription/partner fees through an existing employer or community channel. **Dependencies:** licensed wallet/card/ramp partners, eligibility, consumer protection, recovery and support. **Multichain:** yes. **Substitute:** Felix/Airtm/MoneyGram-linked wallets, MetaMask Card, Tuyo/Fuse, local banks. **Failure:** no affordable acquisition path or better net user economics. **Confidence:** high repeated household job, low new-company advantage. Reject a wallet clone; support does not create distribution.

## E. Founder-level briefs for the top three

### 1. Payment delivery and recovery operations

**1 — Thesis and journey.** Help a multi-provider payout operator get every obligation to a defensible terminal outcome. Example: its Stellar USDC transfer succeeds, but the local payout is held. An analyst opens one case, sees who holds the funds and the provider's reason, obtains missing evidence through a secure beneficiary link, tracks the response clock, confirms return if delivery fails, obtains approval for a new payout, and closes the obligation with matched fees/refund/ledger evidence. This is a proposed journey, not an existing pilot.

**2 — Initial segment and reachability.** Seek a smaller licensed PSP, marketplace/payroll payout business or anchor with multiple providers and enough monthly incident work to justify software. “10,000+ payouts/month” is a screening hypothesis, not a hard rule: a smaller high-value operation may have greater loss exposure. The roles/recruitment plan are in D. Public directories make a candidate list possible; access is not guaranteed.

**3 — Existing workflow and alternatives.** Reconstruct their actual workflow rather than assume spreadsheets. Demonstrated constraints are documented in B. Compare against their current processor dashboard, Zendesk/internal tools and Formance/Modern Treasury/Bitwave. Ask to replay three ugly recent cases through incumbent demos. A missing view alone is inadequate; show a costly unresolved handoff or unsafe repeated manual decision.

**4 — Full product.** Operations console, organization/RBAC, obligation and event ledger, provider ingestion, beneficiary/counterparty portal, RFI evidence, case owners/SLAs, escalations, approved recovery drafts, source-fund/refund lineage, accounting exports and client status API. Later: controlled execution, treasury availability and provider performance. Internal operations include connector monitoring, incident runbooks, security review and customer onboarding; this is not an autonomous recovery service with unlimited liability.

**5 — Hard work versus reuse.** Hard: interpreting conflicting provider semantics, preserving evidence provenance, separating stale/unknown from failed, handling partials/fees, proving refund attribution and preventing concurrent duplicate reissues. Reuse SDK/RPC/indexers, standard queues, a proven ledger where suitable, accounting/support integrations and existing licensed rails. Agents can gather records or draft an explanation. They cannot infer a compliance override or authorize money from untrusted documents.

**6 — Stellar architecture.** Start with native USDC classic payments and read-only ingestion of transaction results, operations, asset issuer, memos/muxed identifiers and relevant SAC events through suitable Horizon/RPC/indexing infrastructure. Match them to customer obligations and provider payout IDs offchain. Do not deploy a proprietary token or escrow just to make the product “onchain.” Later, sponsored account/trustline setup or a narrowly scoped smart account may reduce operational failures. Soroban belongs only where a customer needs enforceable onchain permission/recovery controls and accepts the audit/maintenance burden. Existing anchors handle fiat; optional CCTP handles treasury movement, not beneficiary receipt.

**7 — Privacy/data.** Public: chain addresses, transfers, amounts and memos/events. Private: legal identities, invoices, bank details, RFIs, employment data and case notes. Store private records encrypted offchain, separate tenants, minimize retention, use scoped access and log reviewer actions. Use opaque references; never put documents, names or sensitive low-entropy hashes onchain. Providers/banks still see required financial/KYC information; authorized customer staff see operational data. This is business-data confidentiality, not hidden onchain salaries. No SPP/Confidential Token production dependency.

**8 — MetaMask.** Optional for a human controller already using it to authorize a Stellar mainnet action, through the actual Stellar adapter. It is unnecessary for a read-only pilot and unlikely to drive the initial B2B sale. Use a testnet-capable Stellar signer for any later testnet testing. Do not use MetaMask Agent Wallet as a Stellar executor without new verified support.

**9 — Business model and economics.** Test a paid design pilot, then roughly $1,500–$5,000/month base plus sensible throughput tiers. These are proposed prices. Charge for organization workflow, not merely failed transfers; a per-case fee alone can misalign incentives. At an assumed $50 loaded staff cost/hour, $3,000/month requires 60 hours saved or equivalent quantified loss/float benefit before providing ROI. Connector maintenance, customer-specific rules, financial-data security, support hours and procurement dominate chain gas. Setup fees must cover onboarding while recurring software margins remain credible.

**10 — First ten and first hundred.** First recruit the D interviews; obtain two historical-data pilots and convert at least one to paid use. Win ten within one provider/corridor pattern, measuring reuse and retention rather than building ten bespoke systems. Broaden through payment/accounting implementers and customer referrals only after there is evidence they will refer. For 100 customers, standardized connectors, repeatable security procurement and distribution partners are necessary. An ambassador introduction is an interview path, not a repeatable sales channel.

**11 — Multichain and real Stellar value.** The obligation/case model should be chain-neutral from day one; launch connectors should deeply support Stellar USDC and actual anchor flows. Add Base/Ethereum/Solana/bank records where customers need them. Stellar ecosystem value must remain tangible: production Stellar flows monitored and recovered, fewer onboarding/attribution failures, reused integrations and genuine customers retaining this rail. An arbitrary hash or token adds none. If all paying prospects lack Stellar flows, admit the thesis is not currently a Stellar-core company.

**12 — Defensibility.** Earn switching costs through approved case procedures, historical reconciliation, provider-specific operational knowledge and integrations embedded in client commitments. Cross-provider performance data can help only with contractual rights and privacy-preserving aggregation. The moat is not the agent, smart contract, state diagram or exclusivity of a stablecoin. A funded competitor can copy features; it is harder to displace a trusted daily workflow after repeatable deployments, but that outcome remains unproven.

**13 — Risks and responsibilities.** The customer/regulated partners retain KYC/AML/sanctions decisions, custody, safeguarding, FX, collection and payout authority. Startup initially supplies evidence/case software under data/security agreements. Before adding execution, counsel must map who accepts/transmits funds, controls keys and instructs transfers in each jurisdiction. US FinCEN guidance turns on activities and facts, not the “noncustodial” label; EU transfer services can require authorization even when embedded within another service. [FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf), [ESMA explanation](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-data/questions-answers/2071). Never route around a compliance hold. Other material risks: mistaken identity, double payment, stale provider evidence, API revocation, slow procurement and unlimited contractual liability.

**14 — Smallest falsification tests.** Before a major build, interview 8–10 operators/buyers and reconstruct redacted cases. Predeclare a target: three independent firms show repeated cross-provider resolution work after existing tools; two provide lawful data access and name a budget owner; one pays for the pilot. Compare at least ten held-out cases against existing process. Kill/reshape if burden is trivial, existing tools solve the same cases, no one pays, every adapter is bespoke, or the startup can see problems but has no usable path to resolve them. Do not rescue failure with an AI or privacy narrative.

### 2. RWA distributor redemption and lifecycle operations

**1 — Thesis and journey.** Give an investment app one operating system for servicing externally issued tokenized cash products. Example: an eligible investor requests redemption; the desk distinguishes wallet permission, token transfer, issuer order acceptance, dealing cutoff, valuation, bank payout and actual cash receipt. If an XDR expires or a contract is not authorized, staff know the next action and responsible party. The issuer's legal register remains authoritative.

**2 — Narrow segment and access.** Target a wallet, neobank or licensed distributor already offering two external products and handling support itself. Avoid an issuer that controls the whole journey. Use the D interview plan: current issuer-listed integrations, administrators and actual ops roles. Giant issuers can be expert interviews or competitors; their presence is not a likely first customer or partnership.

**3 — Existing workflow/friction.** Issuer portals/APIs, TA support, bank records, accounting and customer support. Current USDY support-mediated Stellar dealing and Etherfuse preflight/expiry requirements are concrete coordination examples. Spiko's daily operating description also includes fund-accounting and bank steps, not just token transfers. [Daily operations](https://www.spiko.io/blog/spiko-smart-cash-daily-operations). The crucial counterevidence is that issuer APIs already do much of this; the independent workflow must cross issuer boundaries and remain expensive.

**4 — Full product.** Distributor console, investor/service portal, eligibility-status tracking without taking legal approval authority, product-specific order/dealing state, custody/issuer/bank adapters, redemption forecasts, support cases and disclosures, distributions/valuation imports, statements and accounting exports. Operations include revisioned product terms, provider incident handling and authoritative record reconciliation. Avoid a consumer marketplace promising universally accessible yield.

**5 — Build/reuse.** Reuse issuer onboarding and KYC interfaces, TA permissions, custody and accounting platforms. Build cross-issuer event mapping, actual-cash availability, exception assignment and consolidated evidence. The hard part is reconciling legally meaningful holdings and economic events across versions/issuers—not minting a token, scraping an APY or inventing a new investor identity standard.

**6 — Stellar architecture.** Classic/SAC products use issuer/trustline/authorization records; contract-native products such as Spiko need the actual contract events and permissions. Offchain order/register/valuation/bank records establish entitlement and redemption status. Do not deposit assets into a generic Soroban vault without issuer permission. Smart contracts are optional for an approved atomic settlement workflow; they cannot erase fund dealing terms or turn public quotes into liquidity.

**7 — Privacy/data.** Public holdings/transfers may reveal positions; legal-investor mappings, eligibility files, tax forms and bank details stay offchain. Issuer/TA/compliance parties retain legally required visibility. Do not promise that hiding a legal name makes a public position confidential. Selective disclosures should expose only what a particular reviewer needs. Production must work without preview private wrappers.

**8 — MetaMask.** Useful only for already eligible BYO-wallet investors or human operations signers. The adapter can sign a supported mainnet transaction/auth entry; it does not whitelist the investor or make an RWA eligible. Embedded/passkey or existing custody UX may convert better. Test actual issuer/asset/wallet behavior rather than assume all RWAs appear in the wallet.

**9 — Business/economics.** Test $1,000–$3,000/month plus connector/onboarding charges. This is a hypothesis; Tokeny's higher published platform prices are category benchmarks, not evidence for this layer. Small product counts and low support burden may not support it. Data access, bespoke reporting, regulatory/security procurement and liability may cost more than subscription revenue. Avoid relying on undisclosed yield kickbacks or assets-under-management growth.

**10 — First ten/hundred.** Start with two multi-provider distributors and one monthly historical-data pilot, then prove the same order model at another firm. Ten customers may require partnerships with fund administrators/embedded-yield implementers; those partnerships must be earned. One hundred requires multi-issuer coverage, standardized integration and repeatable buyer onboarding, potentially additional financial instrument classes. Do not assume one issuer distributes the startup to all of its clients.

**11 — Multichain.** Necessary because providers and customers use different networks. Stellar remains valuable through native asset integrations, permitted Soroban holdings, actual redemption-to-Stellar payment flows and reusable operational support. If the only useful function is issuer-independent accounting with no current Stellar inventory, it is a general fintech product with a Stellar connector, not a Stellar-first opportunity.

**12 — Defensibility.** Cross-provider servicing workflows, authoritative mappings, term/version history, customer support integration and administrator distribution. Tokenistry/TRES/TA systems are serious threats. A universal eligibility badge, shared schema or dashboard is copyable. Any advantage needs genuine recurring cases and the right to use operational data.

**13 — Legal/security/dependency risks.** Issuer/fund manager and TA own issuance/register decisions; authorized distributors/brokers handle regulated offering/order activities; custodians safeguard assets; banks/ramps deliver cash. Startup can initially build read-only reconciliation/case software independently, with data agreements. Transmitting orders, soliciting investments, advising, exercising discretion or maintaining the official register can change the regulatory perimeter. Tokenized financial instruments are not automatically under MiCA; qualifying transfer-agent activities have US registration rules. [ESMA scope](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-2-scope), [SEC transfer agents](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-trading-markets/transfer-agents). The September 2026 US innovation exemption is temporary/conditional and concerns specified tokenized NMS-stock venues; it is not blanket authority for this fund business. [Order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-09-22/pdf/2026-19296.pdf).

**14 — Falsifiers.** Ask three distributors to reconstruct their last delayed redemption with actual identifiers and contracts. Require two current providers, a cost after issuer/TA support is used, and willingness to buy the operations layer independently of product referral fees. Kill if most problems are one-time integration work, providers give complete service, customers cannot share authoritative data or fiduciary review is the only remaining task. Do not build a whole platform before a paid retrospective order-reconciliation service proves repeatability.

### 3. Multi-entity treasury and cash governance

**1 — Thesis and journey.** Help a holding group preserve entity-level cash controls while placing surplus cash and funding obligations across accounts/providers. Example: each subsidiary keeps its approved operating floor; a controller reviews forecast obligations, CFO approves an eligible placement, issuer/custodian executes, and scheduled redemptions are tracked until spendable cash reaches the right legal entity. Consolidated records support close and audit.

**2 — Narrow segment/access.** CFO or treasurer of a private-market/infrastructure holding group with many operating companies and genuinely fragmented cash administration. Local controllers are users. Recruiting route in D uses CFO/accountant/treasury communities and public customer references, not guaranteed DevRel introductions. Conquest is an evidence/reference case already served by a competitor, not an available pilot.

**3 — Workflow and alternatives.** Multiple bank/fund portals, entity policies, manual approvals and reporting. The Spiko/Conquest case demonstrates a customer committing to an integrated workflow; it does not prove an unmet need for another company. Compete with Spiko Direct/Canary, existing TMSs, banks and ordinary broker money funds. Test whether cross-provider control materially improves on staying inside one existing system.

**4 — Full product.** CFO dashboard, per-entity/currency permissions, obligations/forecasts, cash floors, proposed actions, approvals, bank/fund/custody integrations, dealing/redemption availability, intercompany records and accountant-ready reporting. Operations require implementation, policy configuration, access reviews and reconciliation. Sell controlled cash operations, not an APY ranking or financial advice chatbot.

**5 — Hard versus reused.** Hard: legal-entity boundaries, permissions, forecast reliability, actual liquidity timing and reconciling economic ownership across consolidated books. Reuse bank feeds, ERP exports, licensed investment execution and custody. Do not build a new fund or generic yield vault. Forecasting errors must not autonomously make payroll cash unavailable.

**6 — Stellar architecture.** For groups already using Stellar stablecoins, classic assets handle treasury transfers and eligible SAC/contract tokens represent permitted fund holdings. Soroban could enforce a narrow approved mandate, but offchain policy and existing custody may suffice. Bank balances, invoices, fund NAV and liabilities remain external. Stellar is genuinely core only where actual operating flows or eligible cash holdings settle there.

**7 — Privacy/data.** Group/individual entity views must differ. Accounting, cash forecasts, invoices and beneficial ownership remain encrypted offchain; custodians/issuers and authorized controllers receive appropriate details. Onchain wallets still reveal balances/flows. Do not pool subsidiaries' assets to simplify permissions or claim that internal database privacy hides chain positions.

**8 — MetaMask.** Low initial value for institutional treasury. Use existing custody/enterprise signing controls; support MetaMask only if a particular authorized signer already needs it. Wallet integration will not win CFO trust or satisfy an investment policy.

**9 — Business/economics.** Test a group subscription in the broad $15k–$50k/year range, plus implementation, contingent on entity count and measured burden. This is a proposed test, not an industry price estimate. Recurring margin depends on reusable ERP/bank integrations and support, while adviser/distributor revenue would need its own legal/commercial structure. Yield earned by clients is not startup revenue.

**10 — First ten/hundred.** First interview four group buyers and their controllers, secure one paid retrospective cash-policy/close pilot and use existing execution. Win ten groups through a demonstrable entity/currency pattern and a small number of accounting implementers. One hundred requires an implementer channel, templates, audit/security maturity and additional providers/geographies. None of that distribution is currently secured.

**11 — Multichain.** Bank/multichain from the start. Integrate Stellar where customers already use it, not as a prerequisite for every placement. Expand by entity/provider/accounting system. A fiat-only buyer with no Stellar need should receive the better conventional architecture; that would disqualify this as the initial Stellar-core thesis.

**12 — Defensibility.** Entity-policy configuration, ERP workflows, historical cash/close records and accountant distribution could create switching costs. Existing TMSs and Spiko have strong advantages. The startup needs a specific underserved group/implementation pattern, not a claim that tokenization makes treasury software new.

**13 — Risks/responsibilities.** Customer/adviser sets investment policy; licensed provider distributes/executes; custodian holds assets; banks handle deposits/payments. Startup initially supplies software and approved-action proposals. Discretionary investing, advice, performance fees, custody or intercompany funding services need separate flow/jurisdiction analysis. Other risks: forecast mistakes, delayed redemptions, counterparty/asset risk, entity segregation, long sales cycles and concentration in a few groups. Spiko's introducer model is one documented commercial route, subject to its rules and agreement—not a license the startup inherits generally. [Model](https://docs.spiko.io/embedded/distribute_with_spiko_license/overview/).

**14 — Falsifiers.** Obtain one complete prior-month cash-placement/close workflow; quantify time, errors and cash constraints. Compare it with a current TMS/Spiko quote and implementation. Kill if a single existing product solves it quickly, there is no independent budget for governance or the group has no recurring Stellar flows. This is the strongest conventional-fintech alternative in the top three, but the weakest reason to choose Stellar as core architecture.

## F. Separate Instaward-sized first sprint for the winner

### Official facts checked after company selection

Current rules: there is **no standard open application path**. Begin with consistent participation in an Ambassador Chapter; the Chapter Lead helps scope/refers or initiates the request, must be identified, and cannot bind SDF. SDF decides. Initial awards typically range **$1,000–$5,000 USD value, paid in XLM**. Agree deliverables/amount in advance; scope must fit **30 days or less**, barring special circumstances. Review is typically 3–5 business days, not a guaranteed SLA. KYC precedes payment; disbursement milestones follow the agreed plan. Valuation uses the specified CF Benchmarks settlement-price method on the scheduled payment day. Follow-ons are discretionary, up to the stated **$15,000 aggregate cap** and at most two follow-on disbursements. No award is automatic. [Official Instaward rules](https://github.com/stellar/scf-handbook/blob/main/scf-awards/instawards/official-rules.md).

Participation also incorporates the general SCF rules: adulthood, sanctions/jurisdiction restrictions, lawful activity and relevant Stellar contribution. They include representative/team obligations and language requiring two eligible team/organization participants for listed program activities. Confirm how that provision applies to a solo Instaward with the chapter; do not assume personal eligibility. The founder's citizenship/residency, entity/team status and completed KYC are not established in this research. SDF can impose jurisdiction-specific deployment conditions. [General rules](https://github.com/stellar/scf-handbook/blob/main/scf-awards/official-rules-for-submissions.md). Find current chapter/onboarding routes through the [Ambassador Program](https://stellar.gitbook.io/ambassador-program); chapter contact is not a grant commitment.

### Proposed deliverable: Stellar payout recovery case desk

**One product deliverable:** a runnable, documented operations application that joins Stellar USDC payment evidence to one existing provider's payout/refund records and lets an analyst triage, assign, document and close an exception with an evidence packet.

**Specific pilot user:** one payout-operations analyst and their manager at a business already running Stellar USDC payouts through multiple counterparties. **No pilot is currently secured or named.** Obtain a willing operator, data permission and a redacted sample before requesting an execution sprint. If that access cannot be obtained, do not pretend synthetic examples validate demand; revise the idea/scope with the Chapter Lead.

Initial scope uses public/historical Stellar mainnet reads, **one provider-specific read-only adapter plus a second provider's structured historical export**, customer instruction CSV and refund/receipt evidence. The second source can use a simple CSV mapping rather than a second production API integration. Include actual handoff/recovery cases involving both providers; otherwise the sprint tests a single-provider desk, not the central differentiation. It does not require custody, a new contract, signing keys, real payouts or a production private-payment protocol. A second customer's deployment remains a mandatory company-validation gate.

The application must distinguish obligation status, onchain execution, provider acceptance, beneficiary delivery evidence, compliance hold and refund allocation. “Unknown” is a valid result. Automatic reissue is out of scope; an analyst can export an action proposal and record externally approved/completed outcomes. This is a useful first slice of a larger operating company, not a claim to finish the venture for $5,000.

### Acceptance criteria, agreed before the sprint

1. Import at least 30 lawfully shared, de-identified historical obligations with Stellar transaction references and provider evidence, including at least ten genuine cross-provider handoff/recovery cases. Preserve asset issuer, amount precision and source identifiers. Label synthetic cases separately. If the pilot cannot supply this evidence, explicitly report that cross-provider demand was not tested.
2. Reconstruct each lifecycle in a deterministic timeline, linking business instruction, onchain operation, provider event and available delivery/refund record. No provider event silently overwrites conflicting evidence.
3. Handle at least 12 documented adversarial replay cases: duplicate/out-of-order events, partial return, withheld fees, unknown beneficiary receipt, wrong/missing memo, trustline failure, expired transaction, compliance hold, missing refund record, stale status and attempted second recovery. **Zero false “beneficiary delivered” or “safe to reissue” decisions** in the agreed sample. Missing evidence must remain visible.
4. Analyst can assign an owner, request/attach evidence, set a clock, record a reviewed resolution and export a complete case/ledger packet without any live fund-moving permission.
5. On at least ten held-out cases, compare the current manual process with the desk using a counterbalanced review order. Target at least 30% lower median handling time with no loss of adjudicated accuracy. Report sample sizes/limitations and all failures. This is a pilot criterion, not a promised result.
6. Deliver source, setup instructions, schema, fixture/replay suite, demo and a short operator acceptance record. Remove beneficiary PII from the demo; demonstrate tenant/access boundaries and safe secret handling. Read-only credentials stay outside the repository. No grant-success claim based on fabricated transactions.

### What merits the next commitment

- **Follow-on Instaward:** previous deliverable accepted; a second, distinct sprint proves the same case model on a different provider/customer and incorporates feedback. Eligibility/capacity remains discretionary.
- **SCF Build:** a live user need, repeatable Stellar integration, clear product scope and credible production/security/commercial plan. Check the then-current Build track and outcome requirements before applying; do not assume an Instaward guarantees advancement.
- **Customer revenue:** budget owner pays for continuing use after the pilot, actual operators return without founder prompting, measured value exceeds support/integration cost, and data access is durable.
- **Investment:** several unrelated customers retain/pay, deployments reuse the same core, a distribution channel begins to work, expansion beyond Stellar is real and growth is not grant-funded activity. A demo, wallet connections, positive DevRel feedback or onchain volume alone does not meet this bar.

## G. Unanswered questions and exact evidence to resolve them

| Question | Required evidence / decision |
|---|---|
| Is #1 a company or an incumbent feature? | Three current cross-provider cases that Modern Treasury/Formance/Bitwave/current rails cannot close satisfactorily, plus a buyer paying for the residual outcome. Use side-by-side demonstrations, not website checklist comparisons. |
| Can this founder reach buyers? | Five to ten completed interviews with operating/budget roles, lawful artifact access and at least one paid-pilot commitment. Track failed recruiting too. Introductions remain optional. |
| Is recurring burden large enough? | A de-identified 30–60-day incident sample: case frequency, staff minutes, losses, repeat inquiries, funding delay and current tool cost. Do not substitute total transfer volume. |
| Can cases actually be resolved? | Provider contract/API permissions, approved refund/reissue procedures, ownership of funds/liability and escalation contacts. Visibility without an actionable handoff is insufficient. |
| Will deployment repeat profitably? | Second customer/provider onboarded with reused schema/connectors; record implementation/support hours and security-review cycle. Repeated bespoke projects imply consultancy economics. |
| Is Stellar genuinely core? | Current customer obligations settled through Stellar assets/anchors, recurring incidents and a reason the customer retains that rail. If none exist, choose a multichain fintech thesis honestly rather than manufacture onchain writes. |
| Does RWA ops have an independent buyer? | Two current multi-issuer distributors, actual redemptions/servicing tickets, authoritative records and willingness to buy independent of referral/yield revenue. Otherwise reject backup as issuer integration work. |
| Are issuer/custody/ramps commercially available? | Signed or reviewable partner terms, allowed countries/customer classes, fee/FX/limit schedules, data access, service levels and custody/responsibility map. Documentation and grant funding do not grant access. |
| Can privacy unlock a paying workflow? | Employer/issuer names a concrete leakage consequence and pays conditional on a specific privacy property; deployed revision matches a relevant audit/remediation; production issuer/provider compatibility and recovery/view-key design established. Today's previews fail the production gate. |
| Does MetaMask materially change conversion? | Cohort-specific funnel: eligible user, supported wallet version/platform, funded/activated Stellar account, trustline, signature, completed real workflow. Compare existing wallet/embedded onboarding; do not equate downloads with reachable users. |
| Is agent commerce economically real for the target? | Independent unrelated buyers, paid invoices/jobs, useful delivery and repeat purchases net of testing/subsidies/internal transfers, plus finance pain not covered by Ramp/prepaid credits. |
| What legal perimeter applies? | Exact sending/receiving jurisdiction, customer class, fund flow, key/control map, advice/order/custody roles and partner contracts reviewed by appropriate counsel. No protocol takes these responsibilities. |
| Is the founder personally Instaward-eligible? | Chapter participation/route, current general-rule interpretation for team/solo status, jurisdiction requirements, KYC and an execution-ready scope. No automatic eligibility has been asserted. |

**Recommended decision now:** choose payment delivery/recovery operations as the first *customer hypothesis*. Spend the next discovery cycle obtaining recent cases and testing the independent payer. If that fails, examine the RWA distributor backup; do not build both. Request an Instaward only once a specific operator and concrete execution scope exist. This preserves the ambition of a substantial multichain company while making the first Stellar sprint a meaningful falsifiable step.
