# What substantial company to build starting on Stellar

> **Update, 21 October 2026:** The [Bell / Protocol 30 addendum](../research-03/report.md) checks current activation status, CAP-84/87/88, five company implications and whether the discovery ranking changes. The evidence below retains its original 20 October cutoff.

**Decision date: 20 October 2026.** Research only: no outreach, application, deployment, purchase, wallet connection or transaction was performed. Public documentation, repositories, product interfaces, public ledger records and unsigned venue quotes were inspected. This report reopens the earlier payments conclusion and gives agentic commerce, RWAs, DeFi/liquidity and cryptographic privacy separate consideration.

**Best company hypothesis to validate: a liquidity-aware risk and recovery operating system for businesses offering onchain financial products.** Start with the team responsible for an embedded lending/earn product using Stellar: connect its actual exposures to size-sensitive exit estimates, verified execution constraints, oracle dependencies, issuer permissions and redemption queues; turn findings into approved actions and a tested incident workflow with explicit loss and unavailable-exit states. The company would sell operating control and accountability, rather than an APY dashboard or a risk score.

**Best backup: professional RWA execution and cash-readiness operations.** Sell to eligible treasurers/distributors already holding multiple externally issued products and facing recurring cash obligations; compare primary redemption, size-sensitive market exits and approved facilities, then reconcile actual cash.

**Main reason I may be wrong:** these customers may buy a trusted risk adviser, an existing platform or their issuer's service, with no budget for another operational system. The technology gap is narrower than the ecosystem narrative suggests. Neither buyer access nor willingness to pay this new entrant has been established.

**I do not recommend a major product build yet.** The evidence justifies prioritizing the first hypothesis for a buyer-led test, not declaring it a validated venture. A multichain company with meaningful Stellar deployments is more credible than a Stellar-only company. The most attractive technical intersections—confidential treasury plus bounded agents, and permissioned RWA collateral with dependable exits—currently add dependencies faster than they add validated demand.

## Evidence standard and limitations

- **F — verified fact:** primary documents, source code, public interfaces or the specifically inspected records establish the stated fact. A documented implementation is not proof of security, uptime or demand.
- **V — vendor/author claim:** a provider's account of customers, performance, benefits, adoption or its own experiment. This includes vendor case studies and hackathon submissions.
- **A — user anecdote:** a public user's account, not independently authenticated or representative.
- **I — inference:** the analysis drawn from evidence.
- **H — hypothesis:** proposed product, pricing, interview route, advantage or threshold. All startup pricing below is H, not observed willingness to pay.

Platform status was checked **20 October 2026** unless an earlier source/snapshot date is specified. Metrics change and some web dashboards cache earlier dates. Direct asset reads and venue quotes below are narrow snapshots, not a network-wide census or independent audit. Trustlines are not people; balances are not trading demand; mainnet transactions are not paying customers; awards are not product-market fit. Repositories were read, not executed or security-audited. There were no invented interviews or revenue/TAM estimates.

## 1. Stellar capability and maturity map

### 1.1 Execution, assets and controls

| Surface | Verified current reality | Consequence for a company |
|---|---|---|
| Classic assets / SDEX / native LPs | **F:** classic issued assets support payments, orderbook offers and protocol-native constant-product pools. These are distinct from Soroban contract AMMs. Asset identity includes the issuer, not just its code. [Asset model](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/anatomy-of-an-asset), [classic liquidity](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/liquidity-on-stellar-sdex-liquidity-pools). | Native liquidity and Soroban liquidity require different adapters. A Soroban router cannot simply call a classic pool as another contract. Transaction construction must verify the actual operation/composition path. |
| Soroban / Stellar Asset Contract | **F:** SAC exposes a classic asset through the contract token interface; it does not create a separate bridged asset. G-account balances use trustlines; C-account balances use contract storage. A separately issued contract token is not automatically classic SDEX inventory. [SAC](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/stellar-asset-contract). | Useful for contract settlement of real issued assets, subject to issuer permissions. A token appearing in a wallet does not establish a lending pool can hold or liquidate it. |
| Authorization, freeze, clawback | **F:** issuer flags support required authorization, revocation/limited authorization and appropriately enabled clawback. These powers differ by asset/account configuration. [Issuer controls](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/control-asset-access). | Check the actual asset and every destination contract. Legal eligibility, permission to hold, permission to transfer and ability to redeem are separate facts. |
| Reserves and onboarding | **F:** ordinary classic accounts require two base reserves; the current base reserve is 0.5 XLM, with extra reserves for entries such as trustlines. Reserve sponsorship and fee-bump sponsorship are separate mechanisms. [Accounts](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/learn/fundamentals/stellar-data-structures/accounts), [sponsored reserves](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/transactions/sponsored-reserves), [fee bumps](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/transactions/fee-bump-transactions). | Sponsors can remove user friction, but incur cost and abuse risk. A product must handle activation, trustlines, reserve shortfalls and recovery. |
| Smart accounts / policies | **F:** contract accounts implement `__check_auth`; policies can restrict signers, time, destinations and amounts. [Contract accounts](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/contract-accounts). | Useful for bounded delegation, provided all relevant spend paths are covered. A frontend spending rule or an unrestricted fallback key defeats the guarantee. Policies do not establish that a seller fulfilled an order. |
| Settlement and routing | **F:** classic path payments depend on available offers/pools and enforce chosen input/output constraints. [Path payments](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/transactions/path-payments). | Cheap finality does not manufacture liquidity, enforce an offchain refund or guarantee a fiat exit. |

### 1.2 Money and connectivity

**F:** native Stellar USDC/EURC, documented PYUSD deployments and USDT0 infrastructure provide relevant settlement assets. USDT0's lock/mint and messaging architecture has different trust assumptions from a native issuer balance. Circle now publishes Stellar CCTP mainnet contracts and a forwarder/integration flow; treating it as merely future infrastructure would be stale. [Circle Mint coverage](https://developers.circle.com/circle-mint/supported-chains-and-currencies), [PayPal deployments](https://developer.paypal.com/community/blog/pyusd-on-stellar/), [USDT0](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tokens/usdt0-layerzero), [CCTP contracts](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/references/stellar-contracts), [CCTP integration](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/references/stellar).

**F/V:** MoneyGram's current ramp product supports Stellar and Solana; access to an anchor standard or cash network still requires the relevant contract, KYB, corridor and limits. SEP-24/31 specify interfaces, not universal commercial access. LI.FI announced live Stellar support on 15 September 2026, including Jumper; its claimed enterprise distribution is V, not guaranteed acquisition for a new app. [MoneyGram](https://xramps.moneygram.com/), [anchor explanation](https://stellar.org/blog/ecosystem/fiat-on-off-ramps-and-cross-border-payments-on-stellar), [LI.FI](https://li.fi/knowledge-hub/li.fi-is-live-on-stellar).

**I:** reuse these rails. An independent company has to own a repeated business obligation—accepted purchase, usable investment, controlled exposure or confidential disbursement—not merely expose another transfer route.

### 2.2 Where yield actually comes from

| Product type | Economic source | What a customer really bears |
|---|---|---|
| Variable lending | Borrowers pay interest; utilization and rate models change the rate. Token rewards are an additional subsidy. [Blend borrowing](https://docs.blend.capital/users/lending-borrowing/borrowing). | Credit/liquidation/oracle risk and withdrawal constraints when cash is borrowed. Recursive borrowing can inflate both supply and loans; it does not establish outside demand. |
| LPs | Traders pay swap fees. External incentives can supplement income. | Inventory risk, divergence from holding, adverse selection/LVR, out-of-range capital, rebalancing and hedging costs. Total APR does not equal net return. [Arrakis strategy mechanics](https://resources.arrakis.fi/text/arrakisPro/strategies/overview.html). |
| Blend backstop | Share of borrower interest plus eligible BLND emissions. | First-loss exposure and 80/20 BLND/USDC LP risk, with documented withdrawal queue. It is not insured stablecoin savings. [Backstopping](https://docs.blend.capital/users/backstopping). |
| Government-bond RWA | Interest/valuation from the underlying instrument less fund/issuer costs. | Issuer/custodian/legal risk, dealing calendar, currency/FX and redemption rights. “Treasury-backed” does not guarantee an immediate onchain sale near NAV. |
| Fixed-yield PT | Buy a discounted principal claim redeemable for its accounting asset at maturity; sell future yield rights separately. [Pendle PT](https://docs.pendle.finance/pendle-v2/ProtocolMechanics/YieldTokenization/PT). | Underlying/wrapper/contract credit remains; early-exit price may produce a loss. The fixed claim is not necessarily fixed dollars. [Early-exit mechanics](https://docs.pendle.finance/pendle-academy/optimizing-yields-with-pendle/optimizing-yields-with-pendle-key-takeaways). |
| Leveraged carry | Underlying income/rewards minus financing and execution costs, amplified by leverage. | Rate reversal, liquidation, correlated exits and subsidies disappearing. A vault contract cannot remove these economics. |

**H arithmetic, not a forecast:** a $5m strategy charging 0.5% annually produces only $25,000 gross management revenue before costs. A Stellar-only vault operator needs much more distributable capital or higher-value services to support an independent venture. Creating a new receipt token does not change that constraint.

### 2.3 Agentic demand: supply and demos are ahead of demonstrated purchasing

**F:** Ramp already documents agent identities, owners, budgets, merchant restrictions, approvals, cards, MPP/x402 and accounting. Stripe machine payments already documents refunds, reconciliation, reporting, fiat settlement and Connect. Privy/Coinbase/Crossmint supply wallet policies and cross-rail credentials. A generic “agent finance control center” would compete with these existing surfaces. [Ramp](https://agents.ramp.com/), [Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine), [Privy](https://docs.privy.io/security/wallet-infrastructure/policy-and-controls), [Coinbase](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/agentic-wallet/welcome), [Crossmint release](https://www.crossmint.com/announcement/universal-vault-for-agent-cards).

**F/V:** Parallel offers paid search/extract/task APIs using Tempo MPP and Base x402; this establishes available useful supply, not a disclosed recurring agent-buyer population. Virtuals Butler offers negotiation/escrow/jobs on Base, with beta/ecosystem metrics requiring incentive and related-party analysis. [Parallel](https://parallel.ai/blog/tempo-stripe-mpp), [Butler](https://whitepaper.virtuals.io/info-hub/virgens-hub/butler-quick-start-guide).

**F/V:** human evidence is an existing paid category: Prolific publishes a 42.8% corporate platform markup on participant rewards, task APIs and managed expert evaluation, with a named construction-data classification case. That supports a budget for qualified human work; it also creates a formidable incumbent. It does not prove demand for a new agent marketplace. [Pricing](https://www.prolific.com/pricing), [API](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445138-key-features-of-ai-task-builder), [expert-evaluation case](https://www.prolific.com/resources/building-enterprise-ai-a-guide-to-working-with-human-experts).

**Research evidence:** a July 2026 preprint analyzes Base x402 activity and finds substantial linked/manufactured activity, with classification uncertainty. It is neither uncontested ground truth nor Stellar evidence. Raw agent-payment counts should therefore be supplemented with independent payer ownership, actual delivered work, incentives and repeated procurement. [How Agentic Is Agentic Commerce?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12575).

**A:** public developers discuss funding friction, retries, spend controls and receipts, but some participants sell related products. These discussions are leads for interviews, not proof of market size or new-vendor budgets. [Production discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/AI_Agents/comments/1r86g99/is_anyone_actually_using_agentic_payment/), [paid-tool discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/AI_Agents/comments/1sywivv/if_youre_building_an_agent_that_pays_for_tools/).

### 4.2 Precise threat models for the four serious privacy directions

The following are **target designs (H)**, not assertions they all ship today. “Hidden” means from ordinary public observers **inside the specified confidential core**, not from every participant. Public entry/exit is outside that core. A single-asset contract generally identifies the asset even when it hides amounts.

| Property | P1 confidential disbursements | P2 data-minimized investor servicing | P3 confidential treasury execution | P4 confidential underwriting exchange |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sender identity | Public in confidential-token design; shielded design can hide internal attribution. Employer known to recipient. | Public token address; issuer knows legal investor. | Hidden only within private venue; public fills/hedges can expose it. | Borrower/lender contractual identities known; no anonymous credit claim. |
| Recipient identity | Public CT address; stealth reduces attribution; shielded core can hide linkage. | Public holding address unless separate shielded token. | Private venue can conceal others' orders; counterparty knows own trade. | Known to agreed parties; ordinary payments unchanged. |
| Amount and asset | Internal amount hidden; asset often public. Cashout/unique amounts can relink. | Eligibility proof can hide wealth/source fields; investment amount and fund remain public without separate confidentiality. | Private book/settlement can hide amount/asset; venue/pair and external hedge may reveal. | Raw submitted values hidden from unauthorized readers; approved risk outputs reveal agreed predicates. |
| Balances | Internal balance hidden; ordinary treasury remainder public. | Ordinary fund balance public. | Private positions hidden; public wallets remain public. | Submitted balances protected, original public ledger unchanged. |
| Links / spending history | CT graph public; stealth partial; shielded graph protected subject to correlations. | Pairwise credentials can reduce linkage; fund ledger/register still linkable. | Private settlement protects internal history; public edges/hedges remain. | Case IDs/query results link the borrower over time; source records remain at their origin. |

**Trust, anonymity, proving and recovery requirements:**

- **P1:** amount confidentiality needs correct cryptography/ACL, not a large anonymity set. Hiding who pays whom needs a sufficiently large **effective** set after asset, time, provider and exit correlations. One employer's distinctive monthly batch is not automatically anonymous. Benchmark full batch and recipient-device p95 proving/recovery; no production Stellar privacy cost/SLA was established. Spend recovery, note discovery and historical decryption are separate. Use scoped accountant disclosures and rotate future keys; never promise copied view-key access can be undone.
- **P2:** ZK eligibility can be built with audited app circuits/verifiers now, while ordinary holdings stay public. Credential issuer knows source KYC; freshness/non-revocation/source integrity remain essential. Bind proof to investor action, asset, chain, nonce and time. [Privado verification](https://docs.privado.id/docs/verifier/verification-library/verifier-library-intro/), [revocation](https://docs.privado.id/docs/issuer/issuer-configuration/). A revoked credential does not delete an issuer's statutory register or old disclosures.
- **P3:** private orders, private matching and private settlement are three mechanisms. MPC requires the specified honest-party threshold and may abort; TEE requires hardware/attestation/client-code trust; FHE requires secure KMS/ACL/output release; ZK requires sound proofs and protects a witness only from those who do not receive it. Public hedging can reveal the position anyway. Recovery needs an escape/settlement path without the normal operator, or an explicit availability dependency.
- **P4:** private computation cannot certify complete or truthful borrower submissions. Source-authenticated records, model/version accountability, correction/appeal and default disclosure are the hard product. A hosted prover can see its witness; encryption at rest does not protect against the operator while processing. Query composition can reveal raw data through repeated approved outputs. Test leakage and revocation at the dataset/output level.

**Production decision:** P1/P3 can investigate external mature rails, with exact asset/version/audit/operator diligence. They cannot presently promise native Stellar private USDC using the inspected previews. P2/P4 need no private token just to minimize credential/data disclosure; that also means their Stellar-specific advantage may be weak. No design above is “fully private.”

### 4.3 Harm, demand and the simpler substitute

**F:** Coinbase's 2025 breach account describes leaked identity/contact and balance/transaction data used for social engineering. This establishes harm from linked financial data, not proof that public-chain payroll caused the breach or that ZK would prevent support compromise. **A/V:** SDF describes an unnamed bank's confidentiality objection; this is not a named paid pilot. [Coinbase incident](https://www.coinbase.com/en-mx/blog/protecting-our-customers-standing-up-to-extortionists), [institutional account](https://stellar.org/blog/ecosystem/institutional-privacy-paradox).

**I:** aggregate treasury funding to a licensed payroll/ramp followed by bank payouts already keeps individual salaries off the public chain. A cryptographic disbursement company must serve a buyer who needs recipients to retain/use onchain assets, has a specific public-information leakage problem, or needs a precisely achievable reduction in intermediary visibility. “Payroll should be private” is inadequate differentiation. Toku/Aleo and Fluidkey already demonstrate company-shaped alternatives, although incremental privacy-specific recurring revenue was not independently verified.

Privacy hackathons further challenge novelty. **Warden** won Unlink's private DeFi sponsor category; its source lists important mainnet/multisig features as upcoming and backend mnemonic handling defeats privacy from that operator. **StealthSafes** won Gnosis/Safe sponsor categories and points toward later Fluidkey development. **LocalPro** was a Lisbon overall finalist with credential/private freelance settlement. **Meridian** won a Chainlink privacy sponsor category but distinguishes standard Base mainnet from confidential Sepolia/simulated CRE. Awards do not establish employer customers or full-stack assurance. ## 6. Founder-level diligence on five finalists

These are **H designs**, not products promised by their infrastructure vendors. Each has a distinct payer and commercial outcome. No named organization below has agreed to an interview, pilot, asset mandate or partnership.

### 6.1 C1 — Liquidity-aware risk and incident operations

**Thesis and weekly purchasing reason.** Sell the operating system for an embedded earn/lending team's recurring risk decisions. Initial qualification: a small wallet/fintech already offering a Stellar earn product, allocating across multiple markets or considering new collateral, with a named officer responsible for risk and authority over its own allocations. A Blend pool administrator is a separate adjacent customer with different permissions. Do not assume a wallet can pause someone else's pool.

Monday the risk owner reviews actual exposures and withdrawals due; Tuesday the system tests a new collateral/market against oracle sources, holder permissions, available sale depth and redemption timing; Wednesday an anomaly creates a source-linked case and proposed response. The authorized signer approves only permitted actions. Friday finance reconciles actual balances, pending exits, losses and outstanding cases. They buy fewer blind exposures, less analyst reconstruction and auditable response—not an “AI-safe yield” promise.

**Architecture / genuinely difficult mechanism.** Build separate classic Horizon and Soroban RPC/event adapters, authoritative asset/issuer IDs, versioned contract/admin/oracle dependency graph and source timestamps. Reconcile observations at consistent ledger boundaries; distinguish stale/unavailable data from zero. Keep customer policy and entity mandates offchain with access control. Simulate normal exits at multiple sizes and model hypothetical stress separately; an indicative quote is neither a firm fill nor a reliable stressed-liquidation estimate. Add issuer/TA dealing constraints and eligible-liquidator destinations. Independent deterministic policy evaluates any agent-produced proposal; the agent cannot change thresholds or its own signer rights.

Reuse audited protocol contracts, vault infrastructure, custody, known oracle interfaces and public venue APIs. Hard work is semantic correctness across versions, conservative stress assumptions, liquidator feasibility, false-positive control, dependable incident operations and reconciliation. A polished risk score has little defensibility.

**Assets, custody, permissions and failure recovery.** Assets remain in customer-selected custody/protocols. Initially the startup reads data and records approval decisions; later an existing custodian or tightly scoped customer smart account executes. Any custom policy contract needs independent review. A withdrawal can still fail if loans consume cash, the issuer freezes a destination, a route disappears or state changes. A customer-controlled pool admin may pause/add caps only where its contract permits; users of an immutable pool cannot. Preserve chain/provider status before retrying; support waiting, approved alternate exits and explicit unrecoverable/bad-debt states. Never describe case management as guaranteed asset recovery. Offchain contracts identify who bears software error, investment loss, adviser duties and emergency decisions.

**Privacy and agent role.** Public exposures remain public. Store customer identity, limits, response plans, commercial relationships and incident notes confidentially; this is data security, not private onchain finance. Do not publish a new ranked list of vulnerable borrowers. Aggregated research can use protected compute, but Sentinel shows why a TEE simulation must not be called a deployed confidentiality guarantee. An agent can draft evidence summaries and action proposals using redacted data; independent controls and human signers remain accountable. MetaMask's supported human adapter could sign a customer's specific transaction; it is not necessary for enterprise distribution or evidence of agent-wallet compatibility.

**Incumbent and narrow switch reason.** Stenion's methodology and Blockaid/advisers already cover meaningful risk. The proposed switch is from disconnected signals to the customer's own policy-bound **decision, authorized action and reconciled outcome**, especially realizable exits and issuer constraints. It must beat adding that workflow internally or buying it from an incumbent. An existing funded vendor can copy the interface. Potential defensibility is validated adapter semantics, historical execution/incident data, embedded approvals and trusted operating performance, accumulated across customers. None exists yet.

**Pricing, economics and expansion.** Test $1k–3k/month plus a bounded integration fee, paid from product/risk operations—not a retail APY take rate. Specialist analyst/on-call costs may make this an advisory firm rather than scalable software. Avoid startup-funded guarantees. Expansion could cover distributor risk committees, custodians, lenders and other chains, using reusable integrations and permission models. Stellar retains real exposure monitoring, pool/version coverage, approved customer actions and reconciled production outcomes; a decorative Stellar transaction would not suffice. Venture potential depends on many embedded financial businesses adopting the workflow, not a handful of Stellar DAOs.

**Reach the first 5–10 people.** Build a public list of wallet/fintech products advertising Stellar earn integrations. Beans/Seevcash/Meru and DeFindex's published case/integration materials are leads to qualify, not confirmed buyers. Request 3 COO/CTO/risk owners at actual earn distributors, 2 Blend/XOXNO market operators, 2 treasury/risk practitioners at issuers/curators, 1 custody security lead and 1 independent risk/audit practitioner. Seek possible DevRel introductions to the responsible operating role; use public company/professional contacts if none arrive. Learn their last listing/allocation, last failed exit, authority to act, time spent, external risk budget, current provider and false-pause cost. Include satisfied incumbent users.

First ten customers would require founder-led paid diagnostics converted into repeat workflows; first hundred would require an embedding channel through custodians, vault platforms or fintech software and repeatable self-contained adapters. Those channels are uncontracted hypotheses, not distribution assets.

**Buildable now:** read-only decision/case product, real observed depth, policy checks and retrospective action/reconciliation replay. **Additional dependencies:** buyer data/mandate, independent risk expertise, custody integration, scoped security/legal review before execution. **Not established:** reliable stress-exit oracle, current health of every Blend backstop, or automatic risk-free reallocation.

**Three kills:** (1) 5–10 qualified interviews produce no recurring consequential decision and no budget beyond free scores/audits; (2) actual permissions/data cannot support an approved response, or the required controls are already adequate in the buyer's stack; (3) false interventions, integration/on-call labor or liability exceed the price customers accept. A successful historical incident detector alone does not pass these gates.

### 6.3 A1 — Bounded technical procurement with contractual recourse

Exclude self-payments, internal agent calls, model subscriptions and sellers that have no external customers. A weekly workflow or substantial failed-delivery loss must be demonstrated before choosing the exact task vertical.

Monday the owner approves project budget, suppliers and result criteria. An agent requests an order; new terms/counterparty require a human. Seller accepts deadline/remedy, payment is authorized under capped scoped terms, and the system records progress. Machine checks verify bounded criteria; ambiguous quality goes to the buyer. Accepted work settles and is allocated to the project; late/incorrect/duplicate work opens rework/cancellation/refund with evidence and a responsible supplier. Friday finance reconciles delivered value, recovered funds and unresolved obligations.

**Full product and difficult mechanism.** Supplier onboarding/terms, acceptance templates, purchase order, budget reservation, credential delegation, progress, independent checks, seller response, remedial workflow and project-cost exports. Reuse Ramp/payment-provider/wallet policies and x402/MPP transport; they already solve many controls. Offchain state contains commercial terms and customer data. A mandate binds merchant, task, cap, expiry, network, nonce and acceptance-policy version, independently of the agent's context. The agent cannot approve its own changes. Concurrent orders reserve budget atomically at the application/policy layer; retries cannot create duplicate purchase obligations. Deterministic replay applies only to a defined computable result; “AI says good” is not a universal fulfillment oracle.

**Assets, custody, legal and recovery.** Buyer funds remain buyer/provider-controlled. Delayed release/escrow or seller reserves require agreed commercial terms and a lawful custody/payment structure. A stablecoin payment already withdrawn is not reversible because an API says “refund.” Rejected work can result in rework, seller-authorized refund, contracted credit or an unrecovered claim. Never promise credit insurance without capital/legal authorization. Provider outage preserves order/payment state and known liability; verify before resubmitting. Merchant-of-record, agency, tax, IP/data rights and dispute responsibilities must be explicit. Adding cards/fiat is necessary for existing suppliers, not a concession to avoid Stellar.

**Privacy / MetaMask.** Commercial inputs, instructions and evidence remain encrypted/access-controlled offchain; ordinary hosted agent/LLM still sees what it receives. A commitment onchain must not expose low-entropy invoice details through guessable hashes. Public Stellar payments reveal value and relationships; private payments require an additional rail that is not currently a safe native preview dependency. MetaMask's human Stellar signing adapter may serve an approving user. MetaMask Agent Wallet cannot be assumed to execute Stellar orders.

**Competition / pricing / expansion.** Ramp/Stripe plus supplier support is the direct baseline; Omamori already demonstrates semantic signing controls. The narrow switch reason must be service-specific acceptance and enforceable cross-provider remediation. Test $500–1,500/month plus transparent managed-service pricing only where recovered loss/time exceeds it. Human dispute labor and low ticket size can destroy margin. Expand through reusable acceptance classes and supplier relationships across rails. Defensibility could arise from repeat supplier performance, contractual remedies and embedded project workflows; no proprietary network is established. A funded payment platform can copy generic approvals, receipts or refunds immediately.

**5–10 conversations / first customers.** Cold-build a 30–40-account list from agencies publicly describing third-party research/data workflows and vendors' published customer cases. Target 5 AI/engineering-operations heads, 2 controllers and 2 supplier product/support leads. Ask for redacted recent external orders, failure frequency/cost, current subscriptions/credits, who resolved problems, accepted criteria, supplier terms and approver/budget. Possible builder introductions help reach operating customers but do not qualify demand. First ten customers require founder-led managed procurement in one task class. First hundred would need a repeatable supplier channel or integration into agency/research operations software, with declining remediation cost; both remain hypotheses.

**Now:** historical order replay, supplier terms and a bounded acceptance/claim state machine. **Future/commercial dependencies:** actual recurring external purchases, accepted seller remedies, licensed execution structure and operational SLA. The demos establish implementability, not these missing inputs.

**Three kills:** (1) fewer than three qualified buyers show weekly outside purchases with costly unresolved acceptance/remediation failures; (2) existing support/Ramp/Stripe handles the cases or sellers refuse enforceable criteria/remedies; (3) QA/dispute costs exceed fees, or the only model needs startup-funded guarantees or forced chain migration.

### 6.4 C2 — Issuer liquidity capital operations

**Thesis / weekly journey.** An issuer treasury head buys capital accountability for its own stablecoin/RWA liquidity. Monday reviews target depth, price impact, inventory and redemption obligations; authorizes venue budgets and risk limits. The system proposes range/order changes, checks permissions/NAV and obtains signer approval. During volatility it reduces permitted exposure and flags hedging/exit needs; Friday attributes net fees, incentives, inventory losses and capital consumption against the agreed benchmark. If execution fails or a token freezes, reconcile residual positions and stop affected routes.

**Architecture and hard mechanism.** Classic order/LP and Soroban concentrated-pool adapters; price/NAV/oracle/FX inputs, inventory and fee attribution; strategy simulator; bounded customer-owned execution account; approval/audit and emergency unwind playbook. Reuse venue contracts/SDKs. The hard mechanism is economically superior inventory control after adverse selection, price drift, rebalancing, hedging and subsidies—not displaying APR. Native classic pools cannot be treated as hook-enabled Soroban AMMs. EVM Uniswap hooks/1inch Aqua opcodes do not directly port to this surface.

**Money and risk ownership.** Issuer owns committed inventory, manager gets only agreed permissions, and custodian/customer signs. Startup does not solicit retail pooled capital initially. Market-making, discretionary management/dealing and custody roles require exact legal agreements and jurisdiction analysis. An unwind is possible only into real depth/authorized destinations; no guaranteed exit at NAV. Contract upgrades, reward changes, oracle staleness and offchain redemption availability are dependencies. Keep an explicit cash/inventory fallback; do not chase incentives into a different asset without approval.

**Competition / switching / economics.** Arrakis and Lobster are direct, serious alternatives. Switch only for a measured net improvement on issuer inventory and a dependable operational service across the actual Stellar venues/permissions. Test an issuer retainer of $2k–5k/month plus a net-performance arrangement after a defensible benchmark. Do not charge performance fees on emissions while ignoring inventory loss. The sampled RWA volumes are too small to justify passive fee-revenue assumptions. This could become a multi-issuer/multichain operating company with risk models, execution history and issuer contracts, but might remain a specialist MM service. Copy protection requires demonstrable capital efficiency and relationships, not a rebalance UI.

**Privacy / agent role.** Public positions remain public; confidential internal risk limits and execution plans do not hide posttrade holdings. An agent can propose adjustments under a hard mandate; it cannot change inventory or loss limits. A private venue may conceal orders but adds liquidity/settlement dependencies; do not promise private Stellar LP positions using preview wrappers.

**Reach 5–10 / distribution.** Seek 3 issuer treasury/capital-markets heads with actual Stellar inventory, 2 independent market-making/risk leads, 1 DeFi venue liquidity operator, 1 custodian execution lead and 1 distributor treasurer. Etherfuse/Ondo and public venue/issuer materials are qualification leads; DevRel may introduce, without guarantee. Ask who funds current pools, mandate terms, actual net P&L/reward subsidy, quote/exit targets, holding permissions and how a manager is chosen. First ten mandates require direct issuer sales and a live capital record; first hundred likely require asset-platform/custody distribution across chains, not one Stellar pool.

**Now:** read-only P&L/depth/mandate replay; no capital needed to falsify strategy economics. **Partner-gated:** issuer-owned inventory, actual execution rights, hedging/redemption/custody and legal management role. **Three kills:** (1) no issuer will commit capital and a paid mandate; (2) activity is mainly subsidies/self-trades and net revenue cannot cover operations; (3) strategy underperforms passive/established MM after full costs or fails stress/permission exits. This founder should not launch a fund to discover those facts.

### 6.5 P1 — Confidential business disbursement operations

**Thesis / weekly purchasing reason.** A stablecoin-native services firm's controller wants approved contractor/vendor payments completed without exposing amounts to public observers, with receipts, local delivery, offboarding/recovery and audit evidence. Start a lawful contractor corridor rather than replace employment calculation worldwide. Qualification requires an actual confidentiality objection or costly workaround and recipients who need onchain value; a bank-omnibus solution may already solve the problem.

Finance imports approved invoices and role-separated payee records. Human signers approve an exact batch/limit; an agent only finds discrepancies using minimized inputs. Recipients choose a supported confidential wallet or bank route with explicit leakage disclosure. The system funds and executes the batch, verifies receipt or actual bank delivery, repairs failed routes and gives accountants record-scoped evidence. Lost devices/key offboarding follow a rehearsed recovery process. Public fallback is a new privacy decision requiring payer/payee agreement, not an automatic silent repair.

**Architecture / genuinely difficult mechanism.** Reuse an independently reviewed confidential asset/wallet/rail and licensed payout partners; build batch approvals, recipient activation, privacy-aware route selection, private receipts, exception management and scoped records. Separate identity mapping, spend keys, viewing keys and accountant permissions. Bind spend policy to amount constraints without exposing more than the buyer's threat model allows; encrypted balances can break naïve public-balance spending rules. Supporting key turnover, note discovery, historical accounting and reliable cashout is harder than a private transfer demo.

**Exact guarantee and residual trust.** For the first CT-style design, protect amounts/internal balances; sender/recipient addresses, token and timing remain public. The payer/payee know their own transaction; application sees imported plaintext unless client encryption is real; authorized auditors/viewers see their scope. Public funding/exits and ramp identities remain. A shielded design can protect more internal graph but needs an effective anonymity set and recipient adoption. Ordinary hosted LLM receives any plaintext instructions sent to it. Do not market this as hidden relationships or privacy from every intermediary. Section 4 gives the complete actor/property model.

**Custody, legal and failure.** Employer retains payroll/tax/contractor obligations; licensed provider owns its payout/KYC/AML duties; issuer retains asset controls; wallet/rail roles and emergency powers are disclosed. App security and access duties remain with startup. When RPC/proof/ramp fails, reconcile before retry; a final transfer cannot be canceled through a UI. Spend-key recovery and viewing-history recovery are separate. No master decryption support shortcut. Normal recipient consolidation/cashout must be tested for the exact information the buyer pays to protect.

**Current versus future.** Native Stellar SPP/CT cannot be a real-money dependency today. Base cToken documents native USDC, but exact app audit, issuer support, decryption availability and emergency exits need verification. RAILGUN/Aleo/other rails offer different guarantees and fees; no universal winner. An external confidential rail with transparent Stellar ingress is a multichain-first company, not end-to-end private native Stellar settlement. Stellar's future core role requires a separately reviewed production asset/rail; using it only for public funding gives a weaker ecosystem advantage.

**Competition / price / expansion.** Toku/Aleo, Request and Fluidkey are serious. Current multisig/accountant/offboarding gaps could be tested; they are not assumed permanent or unserved. Test $300–1,500/month plus transparent partner costs for a real repeated batch. Privacy fees, cashout, recipient support, legal operations and independent review dominate gas. Expand to vendor/AP and treasury controls only after paid retention and sustainable gross margin. The moat would be reliable confidential delivery and recipient/corridor operations, not cryptography others can integrate. No evidence yet supports labeling it VC-backable.

**Reach the first 5–10 / distribution.** Interview 3 controllers/finance heads at firms already paying stablecoin contractors, 1 payroll operator, 1 crypto accountant, 1 local ramp operations head, 1 compliance/privacy lead, 1 opt-in recipient and 1 treasury/security administrator. Use public stablecoin-payroll customer stories and professional accounting practices; possible builder-to-employer introductions are a route, not secured access. Ask for last two batches, actual leakage harm, bank baseline, required hidden fields, recipient behavior, incremental budget, audit evidence and disaster recovery. Require two independent paying budget owners, not nine privacy enthusiasts. First ten contracts need one successful corridor; first hundred need payroll/accounting distribution and reusable recipient operations.

**Three kills:** (1) no incremental recurring budget over bank/Request/Toku; (2) normal recipient exit behavior recreates the paid-for leak or recipients reject key/wallet friction; (3) exact asset/wallet/privacy/recovery/cashout composition cannot pass review or costs more than demonstrated value. Never solve the third failure by putting real assets into the Stellar preview.

### 7.3 The decision and what would change it

**Choose C1 for one focused discovery-and-replay cycle; do not yet choose a large product build or raise a fund.** The smallest credible result is a real risk owner saying, with recent artifacts, “I must make this decision repeatedly, my current system cannot carry it through, and I will pay for this bounded operating workflow.” A protocol incident, a clever simulator or an enthusiastic builder is insufficient.

The missing evidence is exact:

1. **Buyer and budget:** at least three independent qualified operators show repeated listing/allocation/exit decisions and costly current reconstruction; at least two budget owners accept a priced pilot rather than offer general praise. Proposed threshold is a decision rule, not statistically representative validation.
2. **Actionability:** one operator identifies its actual custody/admin/signing rights and two recent decisions; the proposed control changes a permitted action or materially improves its evidence. A score the customer cannot act on does not qualify.
3. **Incremental value:** replay the same cases against the customer's incumbent stack, including a satisfied incumbent baseline. Show the missing step, reduced work or avoided bad recommendation without claiming hypothetical money saved as a realized recovery.
4. **Delivery economics:** quantify operator hours, adapter/on-call work, false alerts/intervention costs and acceptable liability. Obtain a price decision after the walkthrough; do not derive price from chain TVL.

**Smallest technical experiment:** build a read-only, source-timestamped decision case for one actual Stellar lending/earn exposure, including canonical asset identity, current oracle/version, pool liquidity, holder/issuer restrictions and a normal exit quote. Replay an illiquid-price incident separately as a hypothetical stress scenario. Exercise stale data, disabled collateral, unknown backstop health, unavailable cash, changed quotes and insufficient permission. Produce an approved/blocked/unknown decision, explicit assumptions and a reconciliation/incident record. Do not sign or submit anything. Public-data findings already gathered can seed it; buyer cases require consented records later.

If no buyer or mandate appears after the bounded qualified outreach, stop C1 and test R1's **actual last three cash decisions**. Do not expand the feature list or add privacy/agents to compensate. If buyers prefer an adviser, provide or partner for that service only if its economics are attractive; do not mislabel a consultancy as proven venture-scale software.

## 8. Small implementation, unanswered questions and evidence needed

### 8.1 Instaward-sized implementation only after the company gate

An approximately **$5,000** implementation could validate **one operator's risk-decision casebook**: one lending/earn integration, reproducible asset/oracle/normal-exit evidence, a policy-bound human decision record and explicit failure/reconciliation states. It is a first slice of C1, not a vault, autonomous trading agent or whole company.

Acceptance hypotheses: independently reproducible reads for the chosen assets/contracts; stale/missing/disabled inputs fail to “unknown/blocked”; no normal quote presented as guaranteed stress capacity; one operator reviews two recent decisions and records whether it changes the workflow; all signatures/transactions remain disabled in this slice. Illustrative allocation: $2,800 engineering/adapters, $900 independent protocol/risk review, $700 operator integration/usability work and $600 infrastructure/contingency. This cannot fund full production custody controls, a broad audit or institutional liability coverage.

The official Instaward rules govern any actual request; an award is neither automatic nor evidence of customers. This report does not assess personal eligibility or submit an application. Proceed only after a named operator and measurable workflow are established. Follow-on justification would be paid repeat use and a second independent integration—not more demo transactions. [Official rules](https://github.com/stellar/scf-handbook/blob/main/scf-awards/instawards/official-rules.md).
