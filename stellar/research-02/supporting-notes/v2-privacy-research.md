# D. Cryptographic privacy and financial companies — reopened investigation

Checked **20 October 2026**. Read-only primary-source research; no outreach, grants, accounts, payments or deployments. This supersedes the earlier blanket privacy-dependent-company rejection in `work/privacy-agent-research.md`. Evidence labels: **verified** means a cited document, code or report establishes that specific fact; **vendor claim** means adoption, benefit or availability claimed by its supplier; **anecdote** is an unnamed buyer account; **inference** is our interpretation; **hypothesis** needs interviews or a pilot. A documented mainnet endpoint is not an independently tested payment or proof of economic demand. Mainnet deployment and safe handling of real assets are separate questions.

## Investment conclusion

**Keep confidential business disbursement operations as a serious discovery finalist. Do not reject cryptographic privacy because Stellar SPP is a preview.** Private ERC-20 transfers, encrypted balances, stealth receiving and private execution exist on other networks with materially different trust models. There are already complete private-finance companies, notably Fluidkey and Toku/Aleo payroll. That establishes competition and an operating category; it does not establish unmet demand or willingness to pay a new entrant.

The finalist should sell a finance team a completed weekly job: **approved contractor/vendor batch → appropriate confidential route → recipient receipt or bank delivery → exceptions/recovery → reconciled, narrowly disclosed evidence**. “A private payment SDK” is a component. The narrow initial buyer is a head of finance at a stablecoin-native services firm with recurring cross-border contractor payouts and an actual confidentiality objection to its current process. Existing employment and vendor relationships can remain visible when the buyer needs amounts protected; hiding the entire relationship requires a different architecture.

**A production launch on Stellar-native SPP/Confidential Tokens with real USDC is not supported by the current evidence.** A multichain-first company using an existing external confidential rail is a legitimate alternative, with transparent Stellar ingress and issuer/ramp visibility explicitly disclosed. Do not describe that as end-to-end private native Stellar settlement. An external rail adds bridge/redemption, liquidity, legal and recovery dependencies. For this founder, the main blocker is access to an operational buyer and a paid workflow, not finding one more privacy primitive.

## 1. What each mechanism actually protects

| Mechanism | Cryptographic property | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Shielded assets / pools | Commitments and nullifiers can hide internal owners, amounts, asset identifiers and internal history; users prove valid state transitions | Deposit/exit edges, small effective anonymity set, app/IP observations and timing correlations remain; some single-asset pools expose asset identity |
| Confidential tokens | Encrypt or commit amounts/balances while preserving an addressable token contract | Sender, recipient, asset/mint and transaction graph can remain public; issuer/auditor rights depend on deployment |
| Stealth addresses | A fresh recipient address reduces public attribution to a known recipient identifier | Payment amount, asset and source are generally public; reuse, consolidation, gas funding or cash-out may reconnect history |
| Selective disclosure | Reveal one record, one claim or a predicate instead of a global history | Already disclosed information cannot be made unknown; a narrowly disclosed note is not automatically a complete source-of-funds proof |
| ZK eligibility | Prove a signed credential satisfies a predicate, with validity/non-revocation checks | A proof does not make the credential truthful, remove legal KYC retention, or hide public investment transactions |
| Private execution / ZK | Prove correct computation with hidden witness; client execution may hide instructions and state | An ordinary outsourced prover sees witness inputs unless protected separately; public output can disclose the strategy |
| MPC | Parties compute on shares without one party reconstructing inputs under the threshold model | Collusion, all-nodes-one-owner, output leakage and abort/availability still matter |
| FHE | Compute on ciphertext; decrypt only authorized results | Key management threshold, ACL correctness, output leakage, compute cost and public caller metadata remain |
| TEE | Run plaintext inside attested hardware, reducing host operator access under hardware assumptions | Manufacturer/firmware compromise, side channels, attestation policy, malicious application code and logs remain |
| External privacy network | Private state is maintained outside Stellar, with settlement/redemption at its boundaries | Stellar public edges and bridge/ramp records are not retroactively hidden |

**Encryption at rest in a company database is useful security, not cryptographic privacy from that company.** If its backend decrypts all salaries or mnemonics, the operator and compromised backend can see them. Similarly an agent instruction encrypted until it reaches an ordinary hosted LLM is visible to that LLM provider.

## 4. Cross-cutting threat model and cost/recovery tests

**Anonymity set.** Confidential-token amounts do not require many users to remain encrypted; identity anonymity does. Measure the *effective* candidate set after asset, geography/ASP, amount range, time and common provider metadata, not total historical deposits. Single employer pools and unique monthly salaries can be easy to correlate. A newly launched entrant cannot borrow the anonymity set of another pool simply by using the same curve.

**Proving and availability.** SPP proves browser-side; CT uses a different circuit system. No defensible production p95 device proving latency/fee/SLA was verified here, so do not invent a pennies-per-payroll promise from Stellar base fees. Solana builds proof context accounts and reclaims rent; cToken depends on external attestation response; MPC adds communication/preprocessing/queue availability. RAILGUN's published percentage fee already makes privacy more than chain gas. Benchmark complete batch with low-memory recipient device, cold recovery, approvals, RPC outage and disclosure export before quoting economics.

**Recovery and disclosure.** Wallet seed recovery must also restore derivation paths, encryption keys, note discovery and spent status. Fund recovery is not automatically tax-history recovery. A view key cannot spend, but it can expose sensitive history forever; use record-level exports where possible, segregated company periods and explicitly authorized accountant scopes. Do not promise revocation of a copied view key or plaintext. A lost-view-key support desk must not quietly become a master decryption service. Verify operator upgrade/censorship powers and emergency exit without an available coordinator/ASP/decryption service.

**Aleo illustration.** [Private records](https://docs.aleo.org/learn/core-concepts/public-and-private-state/index.html) support per-record versus account-level view keys; recipients can decrypt sender ciphertext. [Transaction docs](https://docs.aleo.org/learn/core-concepts/transactions/index.html) distinguish public fees (payer visible), private fees and sponsorship. Crucially [delegated proving docs](https://docs.aleo.org/learn/advanced/delegated-proving/index.html) say an ordinary delegated prover sees plaintext inputs and transition information; encrypted TEE proving adds a different hardware assumption. “ZK” does not mean that nobody generating a proof sees its witness.

**Eligibility.** [Privado verification workflow](https://docs.privado.id/docs/verifier/verification-library/verifier-library-intro/) proves credential predicates/non-revocation without giving verifier raw credentials; [revocation configuration](https://docs.privado.id/docs/issuer/issuer-configuration/) has centralized/offchain/onchain status choices. Issuer still knows original KYC, stale issuer state can accept obsolete eligibility, repeated nullifiers/identifiers can correlate users, and revoked credentials do not delete disclosures already made. For RWA, disclose exactly whether a distribution partner, transfer agent and issuer need the beneficial owner register. Use fresh proof bound to asset, action, chain, nonce and time; no generic reusable “compliant” badge.

### Privacy property matrix for the proposed companies

Legend: **H** hides from ordinary public observers within the specified confidential core; **P** partial/conditional; **V** visible; **N/A** not a ledger-privacy promise. This is a **target architecture** and deliberately not a claim all four exist or can ship on Stellar today. Public ingress/cash edges are described separately.

| Property separately assessed | P1 Confidential disbursements | P2 Eligible-investor servicing | P3 Private execution broker | P4 Underwriting exchange |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sender | **V with CT/Inco**, H only shielded transfer design; employer known to payee | V token accounts; no anonymous issuer claim | P/H inside private venue; V public exchange fills | N/A public wallet payments unchanged |
| Recipient | **V with CT**; stealth can reduce attribution, shielded core can H | V holding address unless separate shielded asset | P/H private venue; counterparty knows own fill | N/A; borrower/lender known contractual parties |

## 6. P1 serious finalist: evidence, complete workflow, distribution and kills

### Harm evidence versus willingness to pay

1. **Verified incident, not payroll-specific causal proof:** Coinbase's [May 2025 breach account](https://www.coinbase.com/en-mx/blog/protecting-our-customers-standing-up-to-extortionists) says bribed support personnel leaked customer contact/identity and balance/transaction information for social engineering; it committed reimbursement of affected deceived customers. This proves linked financial/identity data can cause real harm. It does **not** prove public ledgers caused that incident or that cryptographic payroll would have prevented internal support compromise.
2. **Vendor research:** Chainalysis [August 2026 physical attack report](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/violent-crypto-wrench-attacks-2026/) estimates tens of millions in violent thefts. Public wealth discoverability is a plausible risk mechanism; attributing every attack to onchain wallet tracing would overclaim.
3. **Institutional anecdote:** SDF's [institutional privacy paradox](https://stellar.org/blog/ecosystem/institutional-privacy-paradox) describes an unnamed bank unable to expose customer/treasury/payroll flows. Useful harm hypothesis, not a named paid design partner.
4. **Named supplier behavior and self-customer use:** Toku/Aleo's private payroll work and Fluidkey's company-shaped product show real implementations. Toku's private use with its own team/Aleo is narrower than external employer WTP; Fluidkey headline volume is not privacy subscription revenue. Actual Request payroll/offramp [instructions](https://help.request.finance/en/articles/9187843-how-to-pay-fiat-salaries-and-bonuses-in-crypto) and [API](https://docs.request.finance/salaries) establish the operational incumbent, not novel demand.

**Missing evidence:** a named employer that lost a contract, suffered a concrete salary/treasury disclosure incident, or rejected cheaper standard payouts *and* would pay incremental recurring money for this specific privacy/recovery service. We have no such interview, pilot or price acceptance. P1 qualifies as a serious **discovery finalist**, not a validated startup or immediate production build recommendation.

**Simpler incumbent baseline, inference:** a company can fund a licensed payout provider in aggregate, then send individual salaries by bank transfer. Public chain observers see aggregate provider funding rather than each wage; the employer, provider and banks retain records. Request's existing fiat-payroll instructions demonstrate this category of route. If the sole requirement is “don't put individual wages on a public explorer,” private onchain balances may add cost without improving the completed job. The narrower cryptographic buyer must need continued onchain custody/use, privacy from a specified intermediary, or a disclosure property that aggregate bank payout cannot meet. Traditional expense/AP systems already provide role-based access, approvers, receipts and private bank statements; adding those controls to a token wrapper is insufficient differentiation.

### End-to-end weekly operating design

1. Finance imports approved contractor invoices and a separate identity/contact registry. Assign minimal-access roles, dual approval and budget. Agent drafts mismatch alerts using redacted inputs; it cannot spend or ingest full payroll into arbitrary hosted models.
2. Recipients choose supported confidential wallet versus local bank delivery and acknowledge exactly what remains public. A trial receipt confirms address/key control before live value. Choose corridor/asset based on cashout availability, issuer policy and legal employer/contractor obligations; no unapproved “all countries” coverage.
3. System funds batch using licensed ramp/issuer partner. Public entry is disclosed. Buyer signs approved exact batch with payee allowlist, expiry and idempotency key; emergency signer/recovery roles are separated from read-only accountant access.
4. Confidential route executes with documented proof/attestation/provider dependency. Prefer amount confidentiality if relationship is already public; use shielded recipients only when graph privacy is the actual buyer requirement. An anonymity set and recipient adoption plan are needed for the latter.
5. Recipient receives private receipt and can prove one payout, not whole balance. If paid to bank, partner confirms delivered versus merely transaction submitted. Cashout partner inevitably knows required identity/value; don't promise hidden cash edges.
6. Failed approval/proof/ramp event queues a repair case. Check chain state before retry; never duplicate a batch because an RPC timed out. Outage playbook can wait, cancel unsigned authorization or route approved public payout with explicit confidentiality consent; cannot retroactively undo a final private transfer.
7. Lost device or employee departure invokes a rehearsed noncustodial/partner-assisted recovery path, restores discovery/decryption keys and ledger status, rotates future keys, revokes agent credentials and moves remaining funds under approved controls. No “view key recovers spending funds” shortcut. If chosen rail cannot exit without a down service, this dependency is in the contract/SLA.
8. Accountant receives per-batch matching, fees, FX, proof references and authorized disclosure. Retention and access are logged; broad viewing keys stay segregated. Buyer signs off delivered net pay and exception closeout, not just onchain settlement.

### Standalone business versus feature

The defensible wedge would be corridor-specific employer/recipient activation, repeatable exception/recovery resolution, usable accounting evidence and reliable licensed cashout with well-understood privacy. Standard encryption or wrapping a token is not a moat. An incumbent can add confidentiality; the startup must demonstrate better *completed confidential delivery* for an underserved cohort. Expansion into vendor/AP and treasury visibility management could grow contracts; also competes directly with Request/Toku and brings compliance/partner burden. Nothing here justifies a “VC-backable” claim before retention, gross margin and expansion are evidenced.

Potential partners: existing payroll/accounting integration provider; licensed local payout/ramp; exact token issuer; audited wallet/recovery provider; privacy rail team; qualified local counsel; independent application/circuit audit firm. These are **potential dependencies, no partnerships agreed**. Building one's own custody, bridge and regulated payroll engine simultaneously would make a no-network founder's initial wedge impractical.

### Eight interview seats and recruiting path

No outreach was performed. The founder has possible DevRel/Ambassador/builder introductions, **no assumed customers**. Ask those builders for introductions to the *operational roles* below, using a redacted historical batch walkthrough request rather than a pitch about ZK. Parallel public routes are companies already advertising stablecoin compensation, crypto accounting service firms and published payroll customer stories. Never infer an introduction is available merely because a logo is listed.

| Seats | Exact role / route | What must be learned from an artifact |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Head of finance/controller, two stablecoin-native agencies/service firms; builder→employer finance intro or public finance role | Last two batches, current costs/time, confidential fields, actual objection/incident, approver/budget owner |
| 3 | Payroll/people ops manager at an employer using Request/Toku/standard exchange payouts; public user story or ecosystem employer intro | What current tool fails, payee opt-in/contractor classifications, appetite to change rail, incumbent response |
| 4 | Crypto accounting/bookkeeping partner serving multiple small employers; public professional practice | Evidence needed to close books, whether per-record proof acceptable, cost of global view keys, who pays |
| 5 | Local ramp/payments operations lead in one chosen recipient corridor; builder→anchor/ramp operator intro | Supported confidential asset route, KYT requirements, returns, delivery SLA, freeze/redemption and fee quote |
| 6 | Employer compliance/privacy lead or counsel; same employer intro | Whose identities/amounts must be retained, disclosure permissions, lawful stablecoin pay path; no general legal immunity |
| 7 | Contractor recipient who cashes out weekly; employer opt-in referral only after consent | Device/wallet friction, real privacy concern, recovery expectations, bank receipt timing and costs |
| 8 | Treasury security/IT administrator at another payer | Key offboarding, disaster recovery, signer/viewer separation, required audit scope and production approval |

Interview success is not “eight people like privacy.” Require at least two independent finance buyers to show an actual recurring batch, quantify a current costly confidentiality constraint, identify who can approve spend, and accept a priced pilot contingent on a credible audited/legal route. Recipient willingness and partner route must both work. No promised timeline if introductions fail.

### Three kill observations

1. **No incremental demand:** after 8–10 qualified operator interviews, none shows a costly privacy-triggered blocked payment/incident/workaround, and buyers choose ordinary bank/Request/Toku plus bookkeeping at the proposed price. Stop the standalone company; privacy may remain a feature.
2. **Privacy collapses at normal recipient behavior:** typical payees must exit immediately through identifiable public transfers, unique amounts/time or unsupported banks, recreating the information the buyer pays to hide; or recipients won't adopt required wallets/keys. Stop that corridor/architecture, not hide the limitation in marketing.
3. **No production/commercially viable delivery route:** exact current contract/circuit/wallet/upgrade/key recovery cannot pass scoped independent review; issuer/ramp won't support asset or lawful obligations; outage exit/recovery fails; or external privacy+cashout costs exceed demonstrated buyer savings. Do not use preview Stellar real assets to meet a deadline.

## 8. Agent intersections and MetaMask boundaries retained

Agent permission, confidentiality and correct delivery are separate. Ordinary x402/MPP payments expose normal chain metadata; an encrypted balance can break naïve public-balance limits/verification. [Stellar August 6 Q&A](https://developers.stellar.org/meetings/2026/08/06) makes that compatibility issue explicit. Client-local/private executor must protect instruction inputs *and* a signer must enforce policy independently; hiding a malicious instruction does not make it safe. Audit logs need scoped disclosure; globally readable agent prompts could reveal beneficiaries and defeat private transfer value.

Three identities remain separate: [MetaMask ordinary Stellar wallet support](https://support.metamask.io/de/configure/networks/stellar/) and [SEP43 adapter](https://github.com/MetaMask/connect-stellar); [MetaMask Agent Wallet supported chains](https://docs.metamask.io/agent-wallet/reference/supported-chains/) lists EVM networks, no Stellar native support established; [Stellar AI Agent Kit SCF 37](https://communityfund.stellar.org/submissions/recmbfFnpx6eVThqw) is separately funded infrastructure. None proves distribution, eligibility or demand for the others. Fluidkey's recurring receive/earn/spend workflow is stronger consumer precedent than compatible wallet availability. It also already advertises Stellar ingress; a consumer wrapper needs an actual underserved cohort, repeat job and acquisition route.

Final stance: **reopen and retain privacy seriously, choose confidential disbursements for buyer discovery, and keep cryptographic maturity chain- and deployment-specific.** There is no evidenced safe native Stellar private-USDC launch path in the inspected previews today, and no evidenced incremental paying P1 buyer yet. Those are precise missing facts, not a verdict that private financial companies cannot exist.
