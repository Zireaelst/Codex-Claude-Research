# Bell Protocol 30: PQ security and timing delta

Checked 21 October 2026. Research only; no transactions, deployment, outreach, or partner commitments.

## Verified protocol position

**Fact.** The announced votes are **3 November 2026, 17:00 UTC on testnet** and **23 November 2026, 17:00 UTC on mainnet**. These are future votes, subject to change and validator approval, not completed activation. The upgrade guide was updated 20 October. [Bell announcement](https://stellar.org/blog/developers/introducing-bell-protocol-30-on-stellar), [upgrade guide](https://stellar.org/blog/developers/bell-protocol-30-upgrade-guide).

**Live-state limitation.** A read-only attempt to open the official Horizon root at https://horizon.stellar.org/ failed because the web tool could not decode `application/hal+json`; this research does not independently assert the current live mainnet protocol number. Do not confuse software v30 availability with network activation.

**Fact — CAP-87.** Three host functions implement FIPS 204 pure external ML-DSA verification (not HashML-DSA):

| Parameter set | NIST category | Public key bytes | Signature bytes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ML-DSA-44 | 2 | 1,312 | 2,420 |
| ML-DSA-65 | 3 | 1,952 | 3,309 |
| ML-DSA-87 | 5 | 2,592 | 4,627 |

They accept the full message and a 0–255-byte context; invalid signatures trap. Each has separate constant key-decode and signature-decode costs plus verification cost linear in message-plus-context bytes. The CAP still says **final calibration numbers TBD**. Its prototype estimate is a few times Ed25519 CPU for ML-DSA-65 short-message verification and approximately 16–57 KB transient host memory across variants. These are preliminary measurements, not final fees or an SLA. Larger inputs increase transaction bytes and stored-key read bytes. Contract accounts and external attestations can use this primitive; **network transaction signatures remain Ed25519**. [CAP-87 specification, resource utilization and security concerns](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0087.md).

**Fact — roadmap boundary.** SDF's Quantum Preparedness Plan anticipates native classic-account PQ signers in 2027 and separately identifies consensus integrity as requiring work. The roadmap is an expectation, not delivered protection. Its earlier stage-one text names 44/65; the current Bell CAP additionally specifies 87. Pairing-based ZK systems remain outside the plan's current solution. [SDF Quantum Preparedness Plan](https://stellar.org/blog/foundation-news/introducing-the-quantum-preparedness-plan).

**Security inference.** A verifier does not secure the entire authority graph. A contract with ML-DSA **OR** classical-key authorization can still be bypassed through the classical branch; requiring both avoids that particular bypass but adds operational dependencies. Recovery, guardian, admin, upgrade, attestation and policy-change paths need explicit algorithm and threshold review. Classical admin authority that can replace the verifier can defeat the spending policy. Key custody, backups, signer availability, firmware, KMS interfaces and signed-message binding remain trust boundaries. No end-to-end “quantum-proof Stellar wallet” claim follows from Bell. A relayer's classical transaction envelope is distinct from contract spending authorization; it neither automatically nullifies contract checks nor makes consensus quantum safe.

**Fact — CAP-88.** Millisecond ledger close time removes the whole-second clock constraint; the minimum representable increment becomes 1 ms. Existing `closeTime` equals `floor(closeTimeMs/1000)`. Transaction time bounds, `minSeqAge`, claimable balance predicates, upgrade scheduling and Soroban `ledger_timestamp` retain whole-second semantics. Consecutive ledgers may share a whole-second timestamp. New XDR union arms require decoder upgrades. The CAP explicitly says user-time interfaces should be reconsidered if targets fall below one second. [CAP-88](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0088.md).

**Inference.** A 1 ms representable increment is not a 1 ms block interval, latency commitment, throughput improvement, execution priority or matching guarantee. SDF describes experimental faster-close branches, not Bell-delivered sub-second production service. Performance engineering, validated interval targets, operational adoption and potentially later interface changes remain necessary. **Eliminate a Bell-powered high-frequency trading company thesis.** [Bell timing explanation](https://stellar.org/blog/developers/introducing-bell-protocol-30-on-stellar).

## Competition and evidence quality

**Vendor claim / demonstrated experiment.** Fireblocks published a 1 October 2026 NEAR account rotation to ML-DSA-65, linked transactions, and a custody-control discussion. Its experiment used a software HSM through PKCS#11. It explicitly separates that demonstration from mature hardware integration, threshold ML-DSA, recovery and whole-platform readiness. It says repeatable, auditable migration across account and custody models is the operational problem it is working to solve. This is a strong incumbent threat to a new migration-control company, not proof of Fireblocks Stellar ML-DSA production availability. [Fireblocks experiment](https://www.fireblocks.com/blog/post-quantum-key-rotation-ml-dsa-near).

**Vendor claim.** QuantumSafe markets ML-DSA/SLH-DSA signing, scans and hybrid attestations; its site labels Safe Guard, Migration Planner and enterprise offering as upcoming. Its documented dual signature is an attestation capability; chain-enforced authority must be verified separately. The site and docs have differing custody-readiness wording, so do not infer institutional custody maturity. [QuantumSafe products](https://www.qsafe.dev/), [documentation](https://docs.qsafe.dev/en/introduction). QSafe Wallet is a separate consumer brand describing ML-KEM backup encryption and SLH-DSA; backup protection must not be confused with changing the destination chain's spending authority. [QSafe Wallet](https://www.qsafewallet.com/under-the-hood).

**Fact — enabling supplier, not buyer validation.** AWS KMS announced ML-DSA support in June 2025. A startup cannot claim exclusive access to the base cryptography or build a moat around merely calling a verifier. Specific signer compatibility with CAP-87's pure-interface/message/context rules still needs testing. [AWS announcement](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/06/aws-kms-post-quantum-ml-dsa-digital-signatures/).

**Institutional participation evidence.** BIS Project Leap phase two worked with Bank of Italy, Bank of France, Bundesbank, Swift and Nexi-Colt to test PQ signatures in an operational payment system. It found functional feasibility and higher processing time. This is real institutional experimentation, **not an open procurement, committed startup budget, Stellar customer, or willingness-to-pay evidence**. [BIS Project Leap](https://www.bis.org/project/leap). HSBC says it works with Quantinuum on PQ cryptography including tokenised physical gold; again this is adjacent pilot activity, not an available design partner. [HSBC quantum program](https://www.hsbc.com/who-we-are/hsbc-and-digital/hsbc-and-quantum).

**Demand conclusion.** No primary source checked here establishes a currently open Stellar PQ-security RFP or a signed customer for the founder. Standards, software availability, institutional pilots and quantum-threat timelines justify discovery; they do not establish an urgent Bell-driven purchasing deadline. Avoid repeating quantum arrival dates or treating a PQ attestation as protection against rewriting already-finalized history.

## One serious full-company hypothesis: digital-asset authority migration assurance

**Hypothesis, not validated demand.** Sell a recurring security-control and migration-evidence platform to custodians, token issuers and institutional wallet operators. Initial budget-owner hypothesis: CISO / custody security head, with signing-infrastructure engineering as champion. Start with a paid assessment of an existing Stellar book of accounts and contract authorities, then retain ongoing monitoring as policies, keys, contracts and supported chains change.

The complete workflow would inventory spending and administrative authority; map classical fallback and recovery dependencies; obtain policy-owner approvals; validate KMS/HSM signer compatibility and signature payloads; rehearse recovery and rotation; produce a staged migration plan with proof-of-control gates; reconcile the resulting ledger configuration; alert on unauthorized downgrade or new legacy authority; and maintain evidence for internal risk committees and counterparties. Human-authorized execution could become a later product, but is outside this research. Initially preserve customer custody and integrate their existing signer infrastructure.

**Why Bell changes feasibility.** Native contract verification makes an enforceable Stellar contract-account policy practical to investigate. It turns PQ preparedness from a slideware-only exercise into a possible authority-control test after activation. Bell does not create the surrounding assurance workflow, distribution or customer budget.

**Company economics / moat hypothesis.** Charge paid implementation plus annual coverage by monitored authority/account/integration, rather than per ML-DSA verification. Expand from Stellar authority graphs into multiple chain and custody models, policy-drift monitoring, incident rollback planning and institutional assurance evidence. A reusable control model, tested integrations and accepted audit evidence could compound; a thin SDK wrapper or annual quantum report is a feature/services shop. Fireblocks and enterprise security platforms can bundle this, so neutrality across competing custodians and demonstrable independent checks must matter to buyers.

**Kill criteria and validation gates.** Before promoting above the prior finalists: obtain three interviews with actual budget owners; two anonymized real authority/recovery configurations; a written current procurement trigger and owner; and one paid design-partner scope covering ongoing control, not grant-funded demo work. Reject if buyers only want a one-off report, incumbents already cover the full requirement, or classical recovery must stay unrestricted and customers still demand an end-to-end PQ claim. Technical gates: confirm final production cost calibration; independently reviewed contract policies; signer interoperability; lost-key and downgrade tests; classic-to-contract migration constraints; issuer/asset-control dependencies; clear residual consensus and envelope risk disclosure.

**Relative decision.** Keep this as a conditional research challenger to C1 DeFi risk/incident operations and R1 RWA cash readiness, not a new winner on announcement alone. The strongest overlap is C1's recurring security controls; PQ can be a future module if the same buyer and workflow prove real. It does not strengthen A1 procurement recourse or P1 confidential disbursement merely by association, and CAP-88 does not validate C2 issuer/LP operations through speculative speed. Founder-only status and a possible DevRel introduction confer no secured distribution, buyer access, custody partnership, audit credibility or production support.
