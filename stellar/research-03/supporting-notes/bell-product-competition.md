# Bell company opportunities and competition

Research cutoff: 21 October 2026. Primary public sources checked live. Product ideas, commercial judgments and interview thresholds below are hypotheses, not evidence of paying demand. No contacts, deployments or transactions performed.

## Decision

Bell is a credible improvement to the economics and ergonomics of **smart-account omnibus businesses**, not evidence that a new virtual-account category or customer base has appeared. The strongest incremental company hypothesis is an **operations system for receiving, crediting, investigating and refunding stablecoin payments across smart accounts and fiat partners**. It can start in shadow mode today; W-address receipt attribution becomes an integration advantage after activation. A product that only creates W addresses or parses their events is a feature for existing wallet/custody vendors.

Do not automatically demote prior finalists C1 (liquidity-aware DeFi risk/incident operations), R1 (RWA cash readiness), A1 (technical procurement/recourse), C2 (issuer liquidity operations), or promote P1 (confidential disbursement). Bell modestly strengthens C2 and the payments leg of R1; improves A1's pooled settlement plumbing; has little direct effect on C1's detection/response value; provides no privacy improvement to P1. A new ramp/receivables-operations candidate deserves interviews alongside them, not a higher rank based on protocol news alone. With no owned customers, issuers or channel, the new candidates have the same acquisition handicap as the old ones. Possible DevRel introductions are interview access, not distribution or purchasing authority.

The [announcement](https://stellar.org/blog/developers/introducing-bell-protocol-30-on-stellar) schedules testnet and mainnet votes for **3 and 23 November 2026**, respectively, at 17:00 UTC. These are proposed votes, not activation completed as of this cutoff. Its business-relevant premise is customer-tagged receipts to shared contract accounts. Native ML-DSA verification is another ingredient, while millisecond timestamps prepare for future speed changes; neither creates immediate customer revenue.

Status key: **A** = workflow feasible on mainnet today using existing mechanisms, subject to ordinary integration/security work; **B** = the specific Bell primitive requires Protocol 30 activation; **C** = production reach requires further protocol change or third-party adoption/approval. These labels describe different parts of a candidate, not a claim the full product exists today.

## Four product/company candidates

### 1. Stablecoin collections and payment-exception operating system — strongest validation candidate

**Buyer:** payments operations head/controller at an existing Stellar ramp, remittance platform, B2B payment provider or marketplace; technical buyer is head of payments engineering. Start with an operator that already has funds flowing and costly exceptions, rather than a merchant needing a new checkout rail.

**Repeated workflow:** create customer/invoice route → monitor expected vs received asset/amount → credit internal ledger exactly once → compare provider and chain records → triage underpayment, overpayment, unknown receipt or stuck payout → request approved refund/settlement → reconcile finance export and produce an incident audit trail. Sell the investigation queue, service controls and close-the-books outcome. Integrate existing custody instead of becoming the custodian.

**Bell delta:** W IDs can attribute incoming SAC receipts to invoices/customers within a policy-controlled contract treasury without deploying a receipt account contract for each one. That improves receipt ingestion and operational account management. It does not make refunds reversible or identify a payer's legal identity. A refund to a sender address can be wrong when the original payer used an exchange; verify recipient instructions through the operator's existing process.

**A/B/C:** A: ledger matching, classic G+memo/M receipts, provider status ingestion, exception queue and approval records. B: W destination/event ingestion for shared smart-account receipts. C: wallets, exchange withdrawals, ramps and integrated contracts actually accepting W and preserving its ID; fiat partner access and provider-specific refund execution. Keep normal A routes available until partner coverage is demonstrated.

**Evidence/competition:** [Anchor Platform's SEP-24 integration](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/platforms/anchor-platform/sep-guide/sep24/integration) already documents KYC, received/sent funds, errors, partial refunds and recovery states; [refund execution RPC](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/platforms/anchor-platform/api-reference/platform/rpc/methods/do_stellar_refund) already exists. This is proof of workflow complexity, not proof Anchor users need another vendor. [Modern Treasury Ledgers](https://www.moderntreasury.com/products/ledgers) already covers pooled balances, refunds, reversals and reconciliation; a named ClassPass testimonial describes avoiding manual balance verification before payouts. [Bridge liquidation addresses](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/orchestration/liquidation_address/liquidation_address) already provide permanent blockchain-to-bank/crypto payment routes, including Ethereum routes and Stellar G+memo examples. W does not create this business model.

**Commercial model/moat hypothesis:** platform subscription plus supported corridors/integrations or processing tier; price against measured exception labor and reconciliation delays, not a token fee saving. Defensibility could become reliable provider integrations, historical resolution data and embedded finance controls. None is owned at inception. Avoid a basis-point charge until customers show its value exceeds a workflow subscription.

**First 8 interviews:** 3 Stellar anchor/ramp ops leads; 2 controllers at cross-border/marketplace platforms; 2 payments engineering leads; 1 wallet/custody integration lead. Ask for the last ten failed/late/mismatched payments and the actual resolution trail, existing vendor and budget owner. DevRel can help identify respondents but cannot validate their willingness to pay.

**Kill test:** within 8 interviews, stop if fewer than 3 report a recurring exception class with demonstrable staff work, or fewer than 2 will share redacted records for a shadow pilot. Before building execution, require one buyer to accept a scoped paid pilot or a credible procurement path tied to a measured baseline. Kill the W-specific wedge if their actual senders cannot support W or classic M/memos already solve the problem adequately.

### 2. Policy-controlled pooled accounts for agent/application platforms — material Bell fit, weaker immediate company wedge

**Buyer:** an established agent marketplace, API billing platform or fintech application operating many customer-funded spending budgets; risk/engineering owns purchase, not individual agent developers.

**Repeated workflow:** assign budget/customer deposit destination → reconcile receipt → maintain spendable entitlement → configure allowed counterparties, transaction classes and budget limits → approve exceptions → revoke compromised credentials → recharge and produce customer/billing reports. Full product must own budget administration, enforcement integration and incident response, not merely a passkey wallet.

**Bell delta:** shared smart-account deposit attribution is cleaner at many users. Bell does **not** make each W ID an independently authorized account or enforce its own balance. A shared contract accepting explicit `deposit(customer_id, ...)` calls is already possible today; Bell chiefly adds address-level attribution to otherwise ordinary supported transfers. Direct W deposits emit metadata; they do not atomically credit a tenant subledger through a receiver hook. Application/subledger and audited authorization/policy logic must bind the customer budget to the requested spend; an ID in a deposit event is insufficient. Authorization is an existing capability; W improves the inbound part.

**A/B/C:** A: per-contract smart accounts and existing policy/signing tools, or classic pooled custody with offchain controls. B: tagged deposits into a shared smart-account balance. C: actual agent-payment demand on Stellar, W sender adoption and any customer requirement for turnkey ML-DSA signing/secure key operations. The latter is an adoption dependency, not a complete quantum-safe product delivered by Bell.

**Competition:** [OpenZeppelin Smart Accounts](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/stellar-contracts/accounts/smart-account) already compose context rules, signers and policy contracts, including subscription allowances and thresholds. [Smart Account Kit](https://stellar.org/frontier/projects/smart-account-kit/) already wraps these audited contracts, while identifying its SDK/demo as an unaudited integration layer. [Privy's payments product](https://www.privy.io/payments) advertises Stellar and policy enforcement. EVM [Safe agent allowances](https://docs.safe.global/home/ai-agent-quickstarts/agent-with-spending-limit) already implement capped periodic agent spending. Do not market agent authorization as newly enabled by Bell.

**Commercial model/moat hypothesis:** organizational subscription plus active funded budget/operation tier. Incumbents already charge for wallets and signatures; one shared contract does not remove their offchain service bills. A domain-specific approval/risk workflow may differentiate; generic wallet orchestration is vulnerable to bundling by Privy, Dfns, Fireblocks or the open-source stack.

**First 8 interviews:** 3 platforms already collecting real agent/API payments; 2 application treasury/risk owners; 2 wallet infrastructure leads; 1 procurement/security owner. Ask whether customer deposits, controlled spending, or acquiring customers is their actual bottleneck.

**Kill test:** stop if interviews are mostly pre-revenue agent demos, if no platform has recurring funded customer budgets, or if current wallet policies meet requirements. Demand 2 platform design partners with real payment histories and a specific policy incident before treating this as a company. A single contract creates shared operational/security consequences; customers demanding stronger separation may prefer per-customer accounts despite Bell.

### 3. Marketplace receivables, seller balances and resolution system — viable vertical only with transaction distribution

**Buyer:** marketplace/platform head of payments and finance operations. Suitable vertical must already have stablecoin-paying buyers/sellers; a new consumer marketplace is a separate distribution problem.

**Repeated workflow:** issue payment request → attribute receipts → allocate fees and seller payable → hold/release under platform rules → handle partial fulfillment and disputes → approve refund → pay out sellers → reconcile seller statements, liabilities and cash. The company's value is a vertical's rules and resolution service, not address issuance.

**Bell delta:** shared smart-account collection with invoice/seller IDs can reduce account deployment/management where one contract per customer was otherwise necessary. A receipt tag does not establish segregated escrow ownership, automate milestone adjudication or create recourse. Ordinary SAC payments do not acquire an incoming-payment callback merely from W routing.

**A/B/C:** A: custom escrow/ledger workflows and classic account receipt routing. B: tagged shared-contract receipts. C: marketplace/wallet adoption and the chosen escrow/token contracts accepting muxed destinations. True dispute resolution needs a trusted decision process, evidence and agreed rules; neither CAP-84 nor a shared treasury supplies them.

**Competition:** [Trustless Work](https://www.trustlesswork.com/pricing) offers Stellar mainnet escrow infrastructure for **0.3% per successful escrow release**, free testnet, plus custom enterprise terms. Its [single-release workflow](https://docs.trustlesswork.com/trustless-work/api-rest/deploy) already specifies approver, service provider, receiver and dispute resolver. Differentiate on a specific business process or integrate this infrastructure. Modern Treasury already supports split/refund/closed-loop ledger flows. Bell does not establish a blank market for Stellar escrow.

**Commercial model/moat hypothesis:** recurring platform fee plus resolution/workflow fee if justified. Narrow industry integrations, repeat buyer/seller activity and accumulated case outcomes could compound. Without owned transactions, the startup must win both software trust and marketplace/channel access.

**First 8 interviews:** 3 operators in one target vertical; 2 finance/payments leads; 2 dispute-resolution managers; 1 existing Stellar escrow provider. Inspect actual disputed orders and seller reconciliation, not general enthusiasm for crypto checkout.

**Kill test:** stop if stablecoin volume would need to be created from scratch, if incumbents solve the resolution workflow, or if fewer than 2 operators grant access to historical cases and a pilot. Bell alone does not justify reranking A1 upward: it may improve settlement cost/attribution while leaving the difficult evidence, recourse and customer-acquisition work untouched.

### 4. Multi-entity treasury and RWA distribution operations — R1/C2 adjacency, strongest institutional dependencies

**Buyer:** treasury/controller at an existing distributor, custodian, asset manager or fintech offering customer cash/yield products; include transfer-agent/compliance and security teams early.

**Repeated workflow:** receive cash by customer/entity → resolve subscriptions against eligibility and provider acknowledgement → reconcile customer entitlements to aggregate cash/assets → schedule approved allocation/redemption → track cash needed for payouts vs cash actually redeemable → allocate income/fees → publish statements and exception evidence. This is an operations/reconciliation system; a generic yield allocator is much less distinctive.

**Bell delta:** attributable SAC `transfer` receipts into a shared policy-controlled contract can simplify eligible distribution models. Do not assume CAP-84 supplies tagged `mint` or `transfer_from` paths; confirm each issuer flow separately. It does not produce independent holder balances, holder-level issuer authorization, investment eligibility, fund redemption liquidity or bank access. If an issuer requires individual account records/control, W IDs do not remove that requirement. Confirm each asset's contract-account and omnibus support before assuming feasibility.

**A/B/C:** A: read-only cash-readiness/reconciliation across current provider/account models. B: W attribution on compatible asset flows. C: issuer/transfer-agent acceptance of the ownership model, wallet/custodian support, distribution arrangements and supported redemption workflows. Faster-than-current settlement is also C; millisecond timestamp precision alone is not a cash-availability improvement.

**Evidence/competition:** [Franklin Templeton's Stellar case study](https://stellar.org/case-studies/franklin-templeton) describes a live BENJI operating history, direct holder-list management and USDC subscription/redemption integration with Zero Hash. That proves established institutional activity, not availability of an issuer channel to this founder. [Zero Hash virtual accounts](https://docs.zerohash.com/docs/virtual-accounts) already combine named customer fiat deposit routes, customer ledger balances and optional stablecoin conversion/payout. [Treasury Prime](https://www.treasuryprime.com/banks/account_models) already offers virtual customer accounting with funds in core FBO accounts. These are buyer alternatives and potential integration partners, not evidence of uncovered demand.

**Commercial model/moat hypothesis:** annual software subscription plus integrations/entities and service level. A trusted history of matching issuer, cash and client records could create switching costs. Long procurement and dependence on issuers dominate a small protocol cost advantage.

**First 8 interviews:** 2 Stellar RWA distributors/issuers; 2 custodians/transfer-agent operations leads; 2 treasury/controllers of existing tokenized-cash users; 2 wallet/fiat partner integration leads. Ask to map one actual redemption from request through spendable bank/stablecoin cash, including cutoff and reconciliation failures.

**Kill test:** stop the pooled-account variant if issuers disallow its holder model or buyers demand account segregation. Stop the company variant if access depends entirely on introductions with no operational sponsor/data. Require 2 confirmed recurring cash-readiness problems and one sanctioned historical-data pilot. This can reinforce R1/C2 interview priority, but Bell does not resolve their issuer/channel dependency.

## Competitive economics: what can and cannot be claimed

Current public prices, checked 21 October 2026; differing scopes and chain/features mean these are **alternative procurement benchmarks, not comparable quotes or a price recommendation**:

| Alternative | Public commercial evidence | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| [Privy](https://www.privy.io/pricing) | Free developer tier up to 499 MAU with 50K signatures/$1M volume included; Core $299/month; Scale $499/month; enterprise custom | Embedded wallets, policies and onboarding already bundled; quantify incremental operations value |
| [Dfns](https://dfns.co/pricing) | Basic $8K/year includes 10K wallets, 3 chains and 5 policies; Pro $35K/year, 50K wallets and unlimited policies; additional features/usage vary | A buyer can already purchase wallet controls and reconciliation-related features; not all advertised capabilities imply Soroban/W support |
| [Fireblocks](https://www.fireblocks.com/pricing) | Essentials $999/month, up to 6 months; $1M quarterly outbound volume and 0.20% overage; larger annual plans custom | Enterprise custody/governance relationships and integrations are distribution competitors; verify W support separately |
| [Turnkey](https://www.turnkey.com/pricing) | PAYG $0.10/signature, Pro $99/month + $0.05/signature; enterprise as low as $0.0015/signature, conditional/custom | Policy signing is purchasable; chain support and production fit must be confirmed |
| [Circle Wallets](https://www.circle.com/wallets) | First 1K active wallets free; next all-included tier $0.050/wallet/month, decreasing with volume; listed supported chains do not include Stellar | EVM wallet provisioning is already inexpensive in many cases; no automatic migration case from Bell |

[Dfns' existing Stellar fee sponsorship](https://dfns.co/article/fee-sponsor-on-stellar) covers standard operations with policy checks. Its historical article's Launchtube reference is stale: current [Stellar smart-wallet documentation](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/build/guides/contract-accounts/smart-wallets) says Launchtube is retired and recommends OpenZeppelin Relayer. That same documentation distinguishes audited underlying OpenZeppelin contracts from unaudited Passkey Kit and integration layers; do not equate available code with a complete audited customer product. [Fireblocks lists Stellar](https://www.fireblocks.com/supported-assets), but a chain listing is not proof of current Soroban smart-account, CAP-84 or W support.

EVM already has operational substitutes: [Dynamic/Fireblocks' omnibus-sweep example](https://www.fireblocks.com/blog/how-to-automate-stablecoin-wallet-flows-with-dynamics-omnibus-sweeps) provisions deposit wallets, uses EIP-7702/Pimlico-sponsored execution and consolidates funds. Its [sweep documentation](https://fireblocks.readme.io/reference/sweep-to-omnibus-1) explains intermediate vaults, webhook-based crediting and gas fueling. Bell may avoid a per-deposit sweep/account pattern in a compatible Stellar smart-omnibus flow; it does not make attribution or pooled treasury globally novel. Existing Stellar classic omnibus accounts also already avoid the same per-customer contract requirement. Shared smart contracts can already use an explicit authenticated `deposit(customer_id, ...)` interface; compare that workflow and its sender-integration friction as well as classic M/memos and EVM. Native classic Payment operations are not newly W-addressable, and CAP-84 does not add an outgoing tenant ID. Tenant storage still grows with customers, aggregate SAC balance/issuer controls remain at the contract, and a shared balance write may create contention. These are design constraints, not proven throughput or cost figures.

[Modern Treasury virtual accounts](https://docs.moderntreasury.com/payments/docs/virtual-accounts) are already aliases of shared bank accounts, with ledger integration when banks do not maintain per-alias balances. Its [current pricing](https://www.moderntreasury.com/pricing) is usage/custom by rail and account structure; do not repeat obsolete 2021 ledger pricing as current. Pooled balances plus receipt attribution are longstanding financial infrastructure.

**Honest cost hypothesis:** fewer customer account deployments, sweep operations where applicable, lifecycle/state-management work and some support incidents. Measure these against more shared-state integration, entitlement-ledger correctness, reconciliation, audit and security work. No quantified savings are supported without a buyer's account count, receipt distribution, deployment/rent costs, integration effort, vendor terms and measured exception volume. W metadata does not eliminate customer ledgering, licensing/compliance responsibilities or chargeable wallet-provider operations.

**Defensibility/distribution conclusion:** CAP-84 is public, readily implementable infrastructure. Its largest immediate benefits may accrue to incumbent wallet/custody/fintech vendors with customers. An independent founder earns a company through one painful repeated workflow, privileged operational integration and reliable outcomes. Test this through interviews and shadow reconciliation before building custody, moving funds or claiming a Bell-led ranking reversal.
